\begin{abstract}
\noindent I describe an open-source Python implementation of the Office for Budget Responsibility's macroeconometric model of the UK economy, built for near-term fiscal-reform scoring in the PolicyEngine Macro suite. The emulator transpiles the OBR's 372 published EViews equations into Python, solves them by Gauss--Seidel iteration with a demand-side closure swap, and anchors the baseline to the OBR's November 2025 \emph{Economic and Fiscal Outlook} using the OBR's own add-factor device. In anchored mode the model reproduces the published EFO path with a GDP MAPE of 0.18 per cent and consumption of 0.29 per cent over 2025Q1--2027Q4, hard-gated in continuous integration---though those are one gate rather than two, since every other expenditure component is exogenous or pinned and the anchored GDP error therefore equals the anchored consumption error to the pound. The free-running fit is reported without flattery: of 21 headline variables, 10 are passthroughs and 6 of the 11 actually computed land within band, with company profits 63.3 per cent off---a gap traced to an unpublished OBR calibration constant rather than a live bug. A static-costing bridge injects PolicyEngine microsimulation costings into the model as a household-disposable-income shock, delivering the OBR's own ``static costing in, second-round effects out'' workflow. A worked example---raising the basic rate of income tax by 1p---yields a static costing of \pounds 6.46 billion in 2026 rising to \pounds 7.38 billion by 2030, within the range of HMRC's published ready reckoner, with second-round GDP effects building from $-$0.02 per cent on impact to $-$0.06 per cent by end-2027. That household channel is the only one of the model's four fiscal levers with a behavioural response in it: three further experiments---a \pounds 5bn government-consumption expansion, a VAT-style one per cent consumer-price shock, and a 2pp corporation-tax rise through the user-cost-of-capital channel---exercise the direct levers and show government consumption to be a pure accounting identity (multiplier exactly 1.0000 on impact at any shock size), government investment to be dead, and the corporation-tax response not to converge beyond the twelve-quarter horizon every published result here uses.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Current-vintage note (13 August 2026).} The deployed emulator is
anchored to the \emph{March 2026} EFO, where the anchored GDP MAPE is 0.15 per
cent and consumption 0.25 per cent, and the free-running figures quoted above
(4.48 per cent GDP, 7.49 per cent consumption, 63.3 per cent company profits, 6
of 11 within band) are that vintage. Earlier versions of this paper quoted
free-running errors of 5.70/9.45 or 5.75/9.56 per cent with 4 of 11 within band;
those predate the $OSHH$ ONS level anchor and overstated the model's error.
Sections~\ref{sec:comparison}'s panel~A and the vintage-drift table still
describe the preserved November 2025 anchoring study and say so.
\end{abstract}
