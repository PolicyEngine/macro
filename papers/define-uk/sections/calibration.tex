\section{External calibration and the multiplier}
\label{sec:calibration}

Replication against a publication and calibration against the world are different questions, and a model can pass the first while failing the second badly. DEFINE-UK does. This section reports the second, and then reports the one number from the model that has circulated most widely --- a cumulative green-investment multiplier, 2.25 from the 2027 policy start to 2040 --- with the reasons it cannot be read straight against anybody else's multiplier.

The 2025 growth gap is the one to keep in view: 4.66 per cent against an ONS outturn of 1.31, a gap of well over three percentage points on the single most familiar macroeconomic quantity there is. Unemployment sits about half a point below the outturn, the labour force about a million and a half high, and 2024 emissions some 30 MtCO$_2$e above the DESNZ actuals the model should be starting from. None of this is presented as a defect of the model as its authors intend it to be used --- the manual is explicit that the baseline is not a forecast --- but it is decisive for how the adapter may serve it. It is the measured basis for Section~\ref{sec:deltas}'s deltas-only rule.

\subsection{The multiplier, and how it sits against published ones}
\label{sec:multiplier}

The model's cumulative green-public-investment multiplier is \textbf{2.25} by \emph{our own} computation: nominal cumulative $\Delta$GDP over cumulative $\Delta$SPEND\_GVTPS, the government power-sector spending the policy switch sets, both counted from the 2027Q1 policy start to 2040, on the green-public-investment-plus-green-bonds scenario against its baseline. Three related ratios are reported with it.

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Full simulation, from 2023:} 2.30 ($209.9/91.2 = 2.3009$), the headline the model repository pins. It includes 4.8 of GDP delta from 2023--26, before any policy switch acts; removing that gives 2.25. This paper headlines 2.25 because the served scenario paths start at the policy start.
\item \emph{By horizon, from the policy start:} 0.47 after one year, 0.79 to 2031, 1.40 to 2034 and 2.25 to 2040.
\item \emph{Secondary, per pound of all non-wage government spending:} 1.78 over the full simulation ($209.9/118.2 = 1.7758$, with SPEND\_GVT as the denominator). It was the headline until 15 November 2026.
\end{itemize}

The full-simulation ratios are pinned in the test suite to $\pm0.01$ with their cumulants pinned to $\pm0.5$, and the horizon profile to $\pm0.01$.

\paragraph{Baseline and policy runs use different $\alpha_{PP}$.} The upstream Baseline runs production's own-input coefficient \texttt{alpha\_PPLR} at 0.2748; every policy run uses 0.2762 (printed in each run's \texttt{Parameters-sorted\_*.csv}). No policy switch acts before 2027Q1, so the 2023--26 deltas are this parameter change alone, not policy effects; real GDP sits about $-0.16$\,\% of baseline in 2025--26. After 2026 the two cannot be separated, so the published numbers stand and the range below bounds the policy-only effect (the parameter effect stopping in 2027, or persisting at its 2026 level or share of baseline): headline multiplier 2.25 (the parameter effect stopping in 2027) $\rightarrow$ as low as 2.08 if it persists, and the secondary 1.78 $\rightarrow$ 1.73--1.75; green-investment GDP peak $+0.92$\,\% $\rightarrow$ $+0.92$--1.08\,\%; housing-regulation real GDP in 2035 $-2.3$ $\rightarrow$ $-0.96$ to $-2.3$; fossil-fuel-ban real GDP in 2030 $-3.85$ $\rightarrow$ $-2.65$ to $-3.85$; emissions deltas change by under 0.25\,\%. Sized in define-uk-model \#20; the fix is not yet decided. The multipliers above therefore include the parameter change.

Two labels must travel with it. The first is that \textbf{the number is ours, not the authors'}: no published DEFINE-UK multiplier corresponds to it, and it is a quantity this replication defined and computed, not one it reproduced. The second is that \textbf{it is not like-for-like against any published multiplier}, on four grounds: horizon, denominator, closure and the parameter change above.

\paragraph{Horizon.} Ours is a cumulative ratio to 2040, and it is back-loaded: 0.47 after one year from the policy start and below one for the first five years. Published green-spending multipliers, and the OBR's capital-spending impact multiplier of roughly 1.0, are short-horizon figures --- impact or first-few-year. At those horizons ours sits \emph{below} them; it exceeds them only cumulated to 2040. Setting 2.25 beside a short-horizon number and reading a disagreement is therefore a units error dressed as a finding. An earlier version of this paragraph said a long-horizon ratio is expected to sit above the short-horizon figures; at their horizons the profile shows the opposite.

\paragraph{Denominator.} The headline divides by SPEND\_GVTPS alone: it is the variable the policy switch sets, and it leaves out the induced spending responses. The secondary ratio divides by SPEND\_GVT, total government spending excluding wages: other government consumption plus social benefits plus government gross capital formation plus government power-sector spending, SPEND\_GVTPS. The manual's Table~6 components omit SPEND\_GVTPS, which is zero at the start ($58.03 + 82.8 + 20.76 = 161.59$ against a tabulated 161.6). In the cached GPI + Green Bonds run the cumulative change in SPEND\_GVT of 118.2 is SPEND\_GVTPS $+91.2$ (most of the policy impulse), other consumption $+21.1$, social benefits $+8.0$ and government investment $-2.3$ (green $+17.0$ offset by conventional $-19.3$: the scenario substitutes green for conventional public investment). The secondary's denominator therefore mixes the impulse with endogenous items that \emph{rise} in this scenario, so the direction of its bias against an impulse-based multiplier is \textbf{not established}. An earlier version of this paragraph said that social benefits fall, that the ratio is biased up and that 1.78 is upper-leaning; the cached run contradicts all three, and they are withdrawn. Cumulative $\Delta$GDP over cumulative $\Delta\mathrm{GCF\_GVTG}$ is not a usable impulse ratio either, because green investment is a small, offset share of the impulse. The impulse definition was decided on 15 November 2026 (define-uk-model \#16): the policy instrument alone. The rejected options were $\Delta$SPEND\_GVTPS plus $\Delta\mathrm{GCF\_GVTG}$ (1.94; partly endogenous, and it ignores the conventional offset) and the upstream's real ratio of 2.41 (not comparable with nominal ratios).

\paragraph{Closure.} As Section~\ref{sec:model} sets out, the model is demand-led with Kaldor--Verdoorn productivity (Eq.~(31)) and real GDP not held to a supply ceiling (Eq.~(38)). A multiplier above one over fifteen years is what this closure \emph{implies}. It is not a symptom of an implementation error, and it is not a finding about the world; it is a property of the model one has chosen to run. That is why the number is labelled rather than corrected.

These three caveats are not editorial: they are carried in code as a constant and gated hermetically by a test that fails if the number is served without them.

\paragraph{A reinstated comparator.} Earlier versions of this project's surfaces set the multiplier beside ``IMF green-spending multipliers of 1.1--1.5''. On 13 August 2026 that attribution was withdrawn as unlocatable. The withdrawal was wrong and was reversed on 17 November 2026: the source is \citet{batini2021}, IMF Working Paper 21/87, which reports multipliers of about 1.1--1.5 for renewable-energy investment over a few years. At those horizons this model's ratio is below that range (0.47 after one year, 0.79 to 2031). \citet{batini2014} is a different paper reporting a different quantity: first-year multipliers of 0--1 for advanced economies.

\paragraph{The upstream multiplier table is unusable.} The upstream run also produces its own \texttt{tables/Multiplier\_Summary.csv}, and it is documented unusable rather than quietly ignored. Its impact, four-quarter and eight-quarter multiplier columns are \emph{identical across all eight scenarios} ($-32.22$, $-0.88$, $2.6$), which no correct scenario-specific multiplier can be; and every policy scenario sets a common switch, so the impact column measures that switch and not the policy. Its totals do reconcile once its row labels are read one scenario off (corrected on 6 November 2026; an earlier version of this paragraph said the spending total matched no variable): the cumulative government-spending figure of 104.22 is the cumulative delta of the upstream's real government spending, 104.1 by our own sum, and the cumulative GDP delta of 250.76 is the cumulative quarterly real-GDP delta of the green-public-investment scenario, 250.6. The ratio of roughly 2.4 derived from them is real over real, so it is not comparable with the nominal ratios above. Neither it nor the table's multiplier columns are quoted as results.
