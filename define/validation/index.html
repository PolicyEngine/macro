So the levels are not forecasts and are never served as such: the manual says the baseline “should not be seen as a prediction”, and near-term levels are not competitive with the OBR emulator or the BoE SVAR. Only scenario deltas are defensible, carrying the demand-led caveat.

About this step

The model's cumulative green-public-investment multiplier, on our own computation, is the tile above: nominal cumulative ΔGDP of 209.9 over the cumulative change in ΔSPEND_GVTPS, the government power-sector spending the policy switch sets, of 91.2 — 2.30 to 2040. Over all non-wage government spending (ΔSPEND_GVT) it is 1.78, which was the headline until define-uk-model #16 was decided on 15 November 2026. It is not comparable to the OBR's ≈1.0: that is a short-horizon multiplier where ours is cumulative to 2040, and a demand-led closure with Kaldor–Verdoorn productivity implies a ratio above 1 over that horizon. The denominator mixes the policy impulse with endogenous items: in the cached run its cumulative change of 118.2 is government power-sector spending +91.2, other consumption +21.1, social benefits +8.0 and government investment −2.3 (green +17.0 offset by conventional −19.3). Social benefits rise, so the direction of the bias against an impulse-based multiplier is not established; an earlier statement that benefits fall and that 1.78 is upper-leaning is withdrawn. The headline now uses the policy instrument alone as the impulse, which removes the endogenous items from the denominator (define-uk-model #16). The caveats travel with every served delta (GPI_MULTIPLIER_CAVEATS, gated by test).

Upstream's own Multiplier_Summary.csv is unusable and never quoted: its multiplier columns are identical across all eight scenarios and its ΔG matches the cumulative delta of no variable in the scenario file. The ≈2.4 derived from it should not be quoted either.

About this step

The one open divergence: the pinned upstream code runs below the manual's published Table 4 emissions by −3.5% in 2025 and −10.2% in 2030, widening to 2040 (tile above).

Not an extraction error: the components (EMIS_NELEC + EMIS_ELEC) sum exactly to the total and the baseline is identical across scenario folders, so it is a vintage or calibration gap between the published table and commit 846081a — raised with the authors as DEFINE_UK_1.1#1.

No numeric v1.1 scenario results are published. That is the ceiling

No machine-readable numeric scenario results are published for DEFINE-UK v1.1: the manual stops at the baseline plus scenario design parameters, and the accompanying papers are paywalled with figure-only results. A published-figure replication of the scenarios is not possible today, and every delta this adapter serves carries that status.

So what is gated is design and self-consistency — the switch sets, and the delta paths against the cached pinned run — plus the two coarse FMM-2023 anchors above.

Every known limit of the DEFINE-UK adapter, with the evidence that holds it
LimitDetail
Deltas only, never levelsThe baseline sits far from official outturns, so levels are never served (baseline against official numbers).
Emissions divergenceThe pinned upstream run sits below the manual’s published emissions table, widening to 2040; pinned, raised with the authors (the divergence).
Multiplier runs highOur cumulative green-investment multiplier is not comparable to short-horizon published figures, and the direction of its denominator bias is not established (the multiplier).
Baseline and policy runs use different αPPThe upstream Baseline runs production’s own-input coefficient alpha_PPLR at 0.2748; every policy run uses 0.2762 (printed in each run’s Parameters-sorted_*.csv). No policy switch acts before 2027Q1, so the 2023–26 deltas are this parameter change alone, not policy effects; real GDP sits about −0.16% of baseline in 2025–26. After 2026 the two cannot be separated, so the published numbers stand and the range below bounds the policy-only effect (the parameter effect stopping in 2027, or persisting at its 2026 level or share of baseline): headline multiplier 2.30 → about 2.08–2.25 (the secondary 1.78 → 1.73–1.75); green-investment GDP peak +0.92% → +0.92–1.08%; housing-regulation real GDP in 2035 −2.3 → −0.96 to −2.3; fossil-fuel-ban real GDP in 2030 −3.85 → −2.65 to −3.85; emissions deltas change by under 0.25%. Sized in define-uk-model #20; the fix is not yet decided.
Unlicensed upstream, never hostedThe authors’ R code is fetched at a pinned commit and run locally; hosted tools return run instructions, never results.
The clean-room model solves the whole manual but does not reproduce itThe clean-room Python registers every printed equation of the manual (§3.1–§3.4.7) and solves the whole model quarter by quarter (define-uk-model #19, merged). It does not reproduce the upstream run. Baseline GDP falls up to 44% below it by 2040, mostly through equity-revaluation equations that are not yet diagnosed, so milestone 2 is not passed (#17). Its own green public investment multiplier is about 1.52 (2.30 upstream on the same impulse). The Taylor rule’s inflation input is inferred from the upstream outputs, because the manual prints no equation for it, and two power-sector profit rates are still read from the upstream run (#24). That figure is a diagnostic, not a served result: every served number still comes from the cached upstream run. Where the upstream outputs follow a form that differs from the manual as printed, the model records the form the outputs follow, found from those outputs alone (empirical.py; Code, step 4).
Two cached scenarios are not stock-flow consistentIn the fossil-fuel ban with no forward guidance (2035Q3 and 2036Q1) and with unexpected enforcement (2033Q1), the power sector’s revenue is valued at a different electricity price from the one buyers pay, so the sectors’ net lending does not sum to zero. The other 28 cached files close. The fault is in the upstream outputs. The adapter refuses these two scenarios and attaches a stock-flow check to every result (define-uk-model #15, merged 6 Nov).
