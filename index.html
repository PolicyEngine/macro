02 — the extensions

Six models answer what it cannot.

It returns no GDP, inflation or interest rates. Each model below adds one, and says where its evidence stops. Compare the evidence →

obr-macroAnchored replication

Adds UK macro feedback.

Tracks the March 2026 EFO to 0.15% GDP MAPE with the anchors held; free-running, 4.48%.

boe-svarForecast evaluation

Adds a near-term UK forecast.

Replicates the paper’s GDP decomposition (37.4% vs ~40%), 8pp short on CPI. Against a drifting random walk, no forecast skill survives the 64 tests run.

frb-usSoftware replication

Adds US shock dynamics.

Matches the Fed’s own pyfrbus inside its two releases’ disagreement (~1×10⁻⁸); no predictive claim.

us-hankPublished replication

Adds who responds, by wealth.

Solves Auclert et al. (2021) to four decimals — the headline targets are inputs, not results.

psl-ogCalibration only

Adds decades: work, saving, capital.

+1pp on the basic rate: GDP −£5.0bn (−0.14%), revenue +0.29pp of GDP by 2030. Targets met by construction; no independent outcome benchmark exists.

define-ukPartial replication

Adds UK climate-policy scenarios.

Baseline replicates the manual; scenarios are design-gated and paper-anchored. Deltas only, never levels.

03 — the joins

Where the models meet.

A score can chain members: score_reform accepts microsim, obr, og and og+microsim.

→pe-microsim → obr-macro. The static costing enters the emulator as a household-disposable-income shock: 1p on the basic rate takes GDP −0.02% on impact, building to −0.06%. The impact multiplier is ~1 by construction against the OBR’s published 0.6, so read order of magnitude. Score a reform →
→og+microsim. OG-UK’s long-run wage changes feed a second microsimulation run. The factor is applied flat, so no transition path is represented, and no scored result is published. Open OG-UK →
→Four more members connect the other way — a macro path in, distributional incidence out, all experimental. See every join →

No US macro bridge exists, so a US reform is scored statically.
