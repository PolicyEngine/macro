how it fits together

How the models connect.

The microsimulation is the core; each macro model joins it at one named point, in one of two directions. Nothing connects macro model to macro model.

1

A reform’s cost goes out to the OBR emulator

The cost from the microsimulation enters the OBR model as a change in household income.

Reform → pe-microsimcost · who pays → obr-macromacro feedback → Scored result

Result: 1p on the basic rate takes GDP down, building over twelve quarters — the worked example is on the OBR Overview. Close to the OBR’s own published figure for income tax; the by-construction problem is on the spending lever, and no quarter of the solve converges, so read the scale, not single quarters.

what each one is for

Six models, six different questions.

Pick by the question you have, not by the model you know. Each line is what that model is for, and what its evidence does and does not support.

obr-macro Tracks the March 2026 EFO by construction; weak free-running. Evidence →

boe-svar Replicates the paper’s GDP decomposition, short on CPI; no forecast skill against a drifting random walk. Evidence →

frb-us Matches the Fed’s own pyfrbus inside its two releases’ disagreement; no predictive claim. Evidence →

us-hank Solves Auclert et al. (2021) — the headline targets are inputs, not results. Evidence →

psl-og Targets met by construction; no independent outcome benchmark exists. Evidence →

define-uk Baseline replicates the manual; scenarios are design-gated and paper-anchored. Deltas only, never levels. Evidence →
