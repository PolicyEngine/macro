About this replicationIndependent build, not endorsed

An independent replication of a published academic model, built on the authors' own sequence-jacobian toolkit, not produced or endorsed by them. Results are model counterfactuals, not official estimates or forecasts.

What it solves, and what is imposed

1

The parameters it solves for

The ground truth is the published paper, not data.

About this step6 points
  • β 0.9762739 against 0.976, and χ1 6.4164196 against 6.416. χ1 is solved numerically from a 6.5 starting guess, the strongest single check.
  • Z, α, μp and τ are solved analytically.
  • The seventh, vphi, solves to 1.7135 against a published 2.073: a convention, not an error.
  • This implementation follows the published Econometrica appendix, which divides the after-tax wage by μw. The NBER working paper multiplies by μw instead, giving exactly μw² = 1.21 times ours, 2.0733.
  • Both versions print 2.073, so Table B.III follows the working paper. The two values describe the same steady state.
  • The model repo now asserts the conversion (us-hank-model #15).

Known limitations

Known limits of the US HANK implementation
LimitDetail
Not a forecaster No data, no estimation, no forecast origin: every result is an impulse response around the paper's calibrated steady state. Nothing in this suite forecasts the US.
Stylized instruments only Monetary, fiscal-spending and productivity shocks; the labor tax is endogenous. No transfer or tax-rate instrument, so score_reform refuses model="hank".
First-order responses Hosted IRFs are linear in shock size: no state dependence, no zero lower bound, and a rise mirrors a cut. The model itself is not linear: on the nonlinear transition, a spending rise of 1% of GDP (ρ 0.8) gives an impact multiplier of 0.251 and a cut gives −0.125, against the first-order 0.175. At +2% of GDP the nonlinear solve does not converge. Parameter uncertainty is not quantified — the calibration is a single point.
MPCs below the empirical range The aggregate quarterly MPC is 0.058 out of the default windfall, 25% of mean quarterly post-tax labour income (about $2,400 per head or $6,200 per household). On a refined liquid grid, rebates of $500, $1,000 and $1,500 per household give 0.104, 0.091 and 0.083, far below the 0.15–0.25 that US rebate studies find. The grid explains little of the gap. The main cause is the illiquid account’s adjustment cost: it is smooth and convex, with zero marginal cost at zero adjustment and no fixed or linear component, so a constrained household that holds illiquid wealth puts most of a rebate into that account. Auclert et al. (2021) report no MPC, so this is a property of the calibration, not a replication failure (us-hank-model #6, PR #7).
Spending multiplier turns negative A spending rise of 1% of GDP (ρ 0.8) raises output by 0.175% on impact, but output is below baseline from quarter 3 (impact is quarter 0) while spending is still raised; the 20-quarter cumulative multiplier is −0.015. The labour tax that finances the spending enters the union wage Phillips curve: a higher tax raises the wage unions set, which raises inflation, and the Taylor rule answers with a higher real rate that crowds out investment and consumption. The household and government budget constraints hold along the path, so this is a property of the model, not a bug (tests/test_spending_mechanism.py; us-hank-model #5). No published impulse response gates these numbers.
Persistence of a spending shock The hosted hank_shock has no default ρ for a spending shock: persistence must be given (monetary and productivity shocks default to 0.9). The model’s README quotes ρ 0.8. For the same spending shock the impact multiplier is 0.075 at ρ 0.9 and 0.175 at ρ 0.8, and the 20-quarter cumulative multiplier is −0.032 against −0.015. Quote ρ with any multiplier.
Higher-MPC calibrations Opt-in calibrations aimed at a higher MPC are under development and are not the published default: a discount-factor heterogeneity variant (PR #9) and a median-wealth calibration (PR #10); the options are discussed in us-hank-model #8. No result on this site comes from them.
Approximate distributional output By-quartile consumption splits the aggregate response in proportion to steady-state MPCs — the MPC-heterogeneity channel, not household-level dynamics along the transition.
Calibration, not vintage The parameterisation is the paper's; there is no data vintage to refresh. Changing it would be a model change.
