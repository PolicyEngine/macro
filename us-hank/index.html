Overview Methodology Validation Use it HANK survey
Parameters match the paper; responses not validated. Evidence →
Why

Six of seven solved parameters match the paper, the seventh after a wage-markup conversion. Impulse-response magnitudes are not validated, and the headline targets are inputs, not results.

Good for

  • How a shock transmits: monetary, fiscal spending, productivity
  • MPCs by liquid wealth and the hand-to-mouth share
  • Wage and labour paths for the incidence bridge (experimental)

Not for

  • Scoring statute: score_reform refuses it; PolicyEngine microsimulation costs it
  • Magnitudes to quote: impulse responses are not validated (limits)
  • US spending behaviour: its MPCs sit below US estimates (why)

What it returns

Stylised shocks around a calibrated steady state, with the household distribution attached.

Monetary easing

Raises output, consumption, investment and inflation.

Tax-financed spending

Raises output on impact only: the endogenous labour tax turns the response negative within a year (why).

By household

The aggregate consumption response is split across wealth positions in proportion to each group’s MPC.

Run it with hank_shock from any connected client. The by-wealth split is built in, not a solved household response, so its concentration among households with high MPCs and little liquid wealth follows from the method.

With the microsimulation

Two distributional cuts of the same shock: the model’s and the microsimulation’s.

US HANKwage · labour IRFs → PolicyEngine microsimulationUS employment income → Who bears it

hank_shock_incidence scales US employment income with the model’s wage and labour responses; the microsimulation reports the change after taxes, SNAP and the EITC, decile by decile (every join in the suite).

The distribution is calibrated, not matched to US data: its hand-to-mouth share is about 54%, against about one third in US estimates.

Model card inputs, outputs, horizon, data, access
Answers
How a stylised US shock transmits through a household wealth distribution, and which households drive the response
Inputs
A stylised shock: kind (monetary, fiscal spending, productivity), size, persistence
Outputs
GDP, consumption, investment, inflation, real rate; MPCs by liquid-wealth quartile (out of a windfall of 25% of mean quarterly post-tax labour income) and the hand-to-mouth share
Horizon
Quarterly impulse responses, typically 5 years, around a calibrated steady state
Data
Auclert, Bardóczy, Rognlie and Straub (2021); no data vintage
Uncertainty
None quantified: deterministic, first-order responses
Access
Hosted stylised shocks, CLI, Python package (Code)
Runtime
About 10 seconds cold on a laptop (steady state plus Jacobian, then cached); instant warm
Joins the microsimulation
Wage and labour responses scale US employment income (the join above, experimental)
Status
Partial replication of Auclert et al. (2021): six of seven solved parameters match directly and the seventh (vphi) after a wage-markup convention conversion; steady-state targets are inputs; shock signs are tested, magnitudes are not; not a forecaster; distributional outputs are steady-state statistics and an MPC-proportional split of the aggregate
Cannot answer
Forecasts; detailed tax reforms (the labour tax is endogenous); PolicyEngine reforms; nonlinear or state-dependent dynamics
