What CI gates, and what is refreshed by hand
  • Gated in CI: the replication invariants and the forecast archive's append-only rule.
  • Computed once and refreshed by hand: the 50-origin rolling evaluation and the coverage study.
  • The sign and zero restrictions are imposed by the identification scheme, so that gate protects the software and cannot be evidence about the economy.

How noisy that comparison is

The 68% posterior band is about ±15pp wide, so the 8.5pp CPI shortfall sits inside a single interval: a real gap on the paper's definition, reported as one, but not resolvable at this sample size.

The CI gate uses a cheap ~50-draw configuration whose global GDP share moves about ±6.5pp across sampling seeds, checked against a wide [30, 60]% band. It catches gross regressions; it is not a second opinion on the production run.

About this stepnotes

The comparison uses the paper's own definition: the posterior mean of the per-draw group share of total four-quarter-ahead variance, which leaves about a fifth unexplained.

About this stepnotes

Rolling-origin audit. One frozen origin is not evidence of broad forecast superiority: across 50 expanding-window origins, no variable beats a drifting random walk at any horizon once the 64 tests are adjusted together (test 4). Three attempts to recalibrate the bands after the fact are in the limits.

The svar-unemployment satellite. Excluding the Covid quarters it beats no-change at horizons 1–4 (relative RMSE 0.82–0.99, not significant); on all 73 targets it is level at h=1 (0.99) and worse from h=2 (1.12–1.35). The published path is capped at four quarters. Its specification is on the methodology tab; skill table: unemployment_satellite_validation.json.

4

Against a drifting random walk

No win survives multiple testing; Bank Rate closest

Error ratios for eight variables at horizons 1–8, across the same 50 origins.

Benchmark diagnostics, and what official forecasters report

BoE SVAR against a random walk, computed from our own runs

Expanding-window design: 50 quarterly origins (2012Q1–2024Q2), estimation from 1992Q1, data through 2026Q2, horizons 1–8. A ratio below 1.0 beats the benchmark.

The benchmark decides the answer.

  • A no-change walk on a trending log level forfeits the whole trend as error.
  • So beating it on a price index is close to uninformative.
  • UK CPI's 0.63 at one quarter becomes 0.83 against a walk with drift.
  • At eight quarters, 0.67 becomes 1.03.
  • Bank Rate is the one series that does not trend.
  • It is also the one that improves under the harder benchmark.

Covid dummies matter for GDP, not for inflation.

  • The rolling evaluation omits the six Covid dummies (2020Q1–2021Q2).
  • Every published forecast here carries those dummies.
  • Published specification: UK GDP against drift goes 1.06 → 0.99 at one quarter.
  • At eight quarters it goes 1.12 → 0.95.
  • CPI and Bank Rate move by less than 0.02.
  • Level with a naive benchmark is still not beating it.
  • UK GDP is statistically inseparable from drift at every horizon (p = 0.33–0.43).
  • That result holds in both specifications.

The AR(1) comparison in the source file is not usable at long horizons: 94.6% of its eight-step squared error for UK GDP comes from one origin, because an AR(1) extrapolates the Covid collapse. Every benchmark carries a worst_origin_mse_share diagnostic for that reason.

About this stepnotes

Three statistics answer three different questions and are not averaged or ranked: this SVAR's pseudo-out-of-sample accuracy against naive benchmarks (computed here, quarterly), the OBR's and external forecasters' published real-time errors (cited, annual), and the OBR emulator's tracking of the official forecast (replication, not forecasting).

The closest to a win. Bank Rate at one quarter, 0.79 against a drifting random walk, is the smallest of 64 variable×horizon tests at p = 0.019. Adjusted together (Benjamini–Hochberg), nothing clears a 10% false-discovery rate on any variable at any horizon.

Where this departs from the Bank’s model

Known limits of the replication
LimitDetail
Frozen data edge Estimation to 2025Q1, conditioning to 2026Q2 — the extended sample, not the paper's 1992Q1–2023Q2 window. Results shift with revisions.
Proxied world aggregates The Bank's UK-trade-weighted world GDP and CPI are unpublished, so they are rebuilt as chain-weighted US + euro-area + Japan + China with time-varying UK trade weights.
One ranking does not replicate The paper's prose ranks UK monetary policy as the largest domestic contributor to CPI variance; here UK supply (14.6%) and UK demand (10.5%) both exceed it (8.9%). The paper publishes no FEVD table, so treat this as unresolved rather than a defect; the proxy aggregates are a candidate cause.
Assumed lag length and simplified pandemic prior p = 4 is assumed, not selected; the pandemic is handled by exogenous Covid dummies.
The posterior is mildly explosive In 99.3% of the 10,000 posterior draws the largest root of the VAR is above 1; the median is about 1.004, and only 4 of the 744 accepted draws are stable (counted on the run before the 2026Q2 data refresh; not yet recounted on the 765-draw run). This comes from the specification (variables in levels and a prior centred on a random walk), not from a code error, and the paper’s own Fig. 3C shows the same shape: Bank Rate fades, while the GDP and CPI levels stay shifted and do not return to baseline by h20. Beyond h20 the CPI level keeps drifting, an extrapolation the paper never shows. Long-horizon responses on these pages carry this property (boe-var-model #11).
No inflation anchor With a unit root and no 2% target in the model, the CPI forecast settles near recent inflation (about 3%), not the target. Re-estimated on 1992Q1–2019Q4 it settles at 2.0%. Read a medium-term CPI gap to the OBR or the Bank as this artefact, not a forecast of overshoot (forecast_vs_boe_obr.md).
No after-the-fact fix brings the 90% band to 90% Scored held-out, the raw 90% band covers about 77% of outturns. Three recalibrations each fall short: per-horizon rescaling factors 82.7% (82.9% on year-on-year growth) (#19), conformal factors 83.2% on the same specification (#20), and Lenza–Primiceri Covid volatility scaling 81.2%, or 86.9% combined with the rescaling (#21). The misses cluster in 2019–22. Read the 90% fan as under-covering, not as a 90% probability; the remaining candidate fix is time-varying volatility (boe-var-model #12).
Rate-shock response above the Bank of England’s estimate Per 25bp monetary policy shock, GDP is about 0.40% lower at h8 (posterior median; counted on the 744-draw run before the 2026Q2 refresh, not yet recounted). Scaled linearly to 100bp that is about 1.6%, above the Bank of England’s published range for a 1pp rise (the benchmark). The experiments differ: here the rate path is persistent rather than held for one year, and GDP is already near its trough on impact rather than after about a year (boe-var-model #11).
Sign-restriction critiques apply Pointwise medians mix structural models (Fry–Pagan); the Haar prior over rotations is informative about impulse responses (Baumeister–Hamilton). These hit the Bank's own outputs identically.

Every deviation is listed in the repository's docs/methodology.md.
