Sample and specification

Estimation covers the inflation-targeting era, 1992Q1–2025Q1, in both the paper replication and the hosted adapter — in levels, with p=4 lags and exogenous Covid dummies for 2020Q1–2021Q2.

Conditioning versus estimation

The hosted forecast conditions on observations through 2026Q2 without re-estimating coefficients on them.

The posterior

The output is a posterior over the reduced-form coefficients Π and error covariance Σ; every downstream object — impulse responses, decompositions, fan charts — is a posterior distribution, not a point estimate, and every one is reported with credible bands.

Identification: naming the shocks

Identification follows Arias–Rubio-Ramírez–Waggoner (2018); sign restrictions name six structural shocks: world demand, world energy, world supply, UK demand, UK supply, and UK monetary policy.

The hosted run

The hosted forecast conditions on the current vintage, data through 2026Q2, and retains the 2025Q1 coefficient-estimation endpoint. Its default 2,000-draw run (159 accepted, ESS 48.7) is below the 100 at which the response warns of weak inference. The current-outlook chart on the overview uses a larger 5,600-draw run of the same pipeline (385 accepted, ESS 176.3).

5

The svar-unemployment satellite

The unemployment rate is not one of the eight VAR variables, so the published unemployment outlook comes from a separate satellite that leaves the replication core untouched: an estimated GDP-growth-to-unemployment mapping that turns the VAR's UK GDP growth forecast into a path for the ONS unemployment rate (MGSX).

The regression
The mapping (dynamic)
Δut=α+βgt+γΔut−1+εt

How it is fitted

The quarterly change in unemployment is regressed by OLS on year-on-year GDP growth and its own lag, over the VAR's 1992Q1–2025Q1 sample. The furlough quarters (2020Q1–2021Q2) are dummied out: GDP printed ~−20% year on year while unemployment barely moved, and including them attenuates the growth coefficient about threefold (β −0.047 → −0.016).

How the bands work

Conditional on the fit, the mapping is monotone decreasing in GDP growth, so the GDP median and bands pass through with the bounds swapped: high growth means low unemployment. The bands carry GDP-forecast uncertainty only, no regression residual uncertainty, so they are a lower bound on the true uncertainty.

The published path is capped at four quarters, where the satellite stops beating a no-change benchmark in the 73-origin rolling test (origins 2005Q1 to 2023Q1).
