Band calibration and the EFO comparison
  • The October 2026 EFO was fixed on 28 Oct, before the 12 Nov round, on less data: the charts show where views differ. The forecast evaluation is on the BoE SVAR validation page.
  • A round archived after an outturn is drawn beside it but never scored on it.
  • GDP and CPI bands are rescaled per horizon to measured coverage: on average they under-cover, but UK GDP over-covers at short horizons (68% factor 0.85 one quarter ahead, 1.13 at eight). Factors: band_calibration.json.
  • Factors come from the model shown (with its Covid dummies) over 50 overlapping origins, so they are imprecise beyond two decimals.
  • The EFO growth path is four-quarter growth of Table 1.1 real GDP in the October 2026 EFO detailed economy tables.
  • Raw quantiles stay untouched in the round files. Re-calibrated numbers are tabled under each fan; raw medians and bands are on the Growth and Inflation tabs. The BoE MPR and the survey of external forecasters are in the official CSVs.

Data vintages and scoring inputs

The data edge is the last observation the model was allowed to see; outturns are read from the dated, never-edited snapshots in the data store.

A CI job fails any pull request that edits or deletes a round, so a correction creates a new round and the superseded one stays scored.

Scores are computed from outturns.json, which is vintage-versioned so revisions never silently rewrite a score, and written to scorecard.json; the method is in the README.

Method and limits

What this record does not show, and how it is kept.

  • Too few to judge: no skill claim before double figures, a year or more away.
  • Bands run narrow in hindsight tests, worse with horizon; open fans are re-calibrated.
  • Unemployment bands understate risk by construction: GDP uncertainty only (round file).
  • CPI does not beat a drifting random walk; no gain survives multiple-testing correction.
  • Three variables, no fixed calendar; coefficients are not re-estimated every round.
  • Every row here is BoE SVAR; the microsimulation does not forecast. How a score is built
How we score six rules, each enforced in code
  1. Archive firstThe round's JSON is committed before the outturn is published (the first round is the exception).
  2. Never editCI rejects any edit or deletion; a correction is a new round.
  3. No hindsightA round archived after the outturn's snapshot is never scored on it.
  4. Dated outturnsRead from a dated ONS snapshot; revisions are appended, never overwritten.
  5. Error and coverageSigned error of the median; coverage of the bands as archived.
  6. BenchmarksBeside a no-change forecast and any published path for that quarter.
  • Archive and freeze: one JSON file per model under forecasts/rounds/<date>/, with median, 68% and 90% bands, data edge and source hash. The forecast-archive workflow fails any PR that edits, renames or deletes one, so the Git timestamp proves when the claim was made.
  • No hindsight: score.py scores a period only if the round's archive date is on or before the outturn's snapshot date; later rounds covering the quarter are never scored on it. The test is by date. It matters only for the first round, archived after the CPI release from a file committed before it (archive).
  • Outturns: ingest_outturns.py converts stored ONS series to the forecasts' units (GDP growth from the ABMI level; CPI and unemployment as published) and appends each value with its snapshot date. Revisions are appended; the scoreboard uses the newest, and earlier scores stay reproducible from outturns.json.
  • Errors and bands: error is the archived median minus the outturn (positive means it ran high), kept signed because bias and random misses differ. Coverage uses the bands as archived, not the re-calibrated bands in the open-round charts.
  • Benchmarks: a no-change forecast (the last outturn at the data edge, held flat), plus any stored quarterly path published before the round: BoE MPR (Feb and Nov 2026), OBR EFO (Mar and Oct 2026) and the BoE survey of external forecasters (Apr 2026; the Oct 2026 survey is stored but scores no quarter yet), from the official CSVs. Everything lands in scorecard.json, generated, never edited by hand.
  • Why publish this early: it makes early failures part of the evidence. Bank Rate comes closest to an accuracy gain, which does not survive the multiple-testing adjustment; it is not forecast here yet. Rounds follow data-edge refreshes.
