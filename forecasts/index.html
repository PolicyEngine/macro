Band calibration and the EFO comparison

The EFO was fixed in March and these rounds later, on more data, so the charts show where the views differ; the forecast evaluation is on the BoE SVAR validation page. A round archived after an outturn is drawn beside it but never scored on it.

The GDP and CPI bands are rescaled per horizon to the coverage measured on the validation page — which widens some and narrows others: averaged across variables the bands under-cover, but UK GDP over-covers at short horizons, so its 68% factor is 0.85 at one quarter ahead and 1.13 at eight, measured on year-on-year growth like the fan itself (factors in band_calibration.json). Those factors are measured on the model that is actually shown, with the six Covid dummies; the earlier set came from a coverage run estimated without them, and narrowed the GDP fan by 36% at one quarter ahead where the published model wants 14%. They are quoted to four decimals because that is the arithmetic of the run, not the precision of the estimate — 50 overlapping origins carry a standard error that moves k far more than its fourth decimal. The raw quantiles stay untouched in the round artifacts. The re-calibrated numbers are tabled under each fan; the quarter-by-quarter medians and raw bands are tabled on the Growth and Inflation tabs, and in the round artifacts below; the BoE MPR central path and the external forecasters' survey are in the committed official CSVs.

Data vintages and scoring inputs

The data edge is the last observation the model was allowed to see; outturns are read from the dated, never-edited snapshots in the data store.

A CI job fails any pull request that edits or deletes a round, so a correction creates a new round and the superseded one stays scored.

Scores are computed from outturns.json, which is vintage-versioned so revisions never silently rewrite a score, and written to scorecard.json; the method is in the README.

Method and limits

What this record does not show, and how it is kept.

  • Too few to judge. No skill claim until scores reach double figures, a year or more away.
  • Bands run narrow in hindsight tests, worse with horizon; open fans are re-calibrated.
  • Unemployment bands understate risk by construction: GDP uncertainty only (round file).
  • CPI does not beat a drifting random walk; no gain survives multiple-testing correction.
  • Three variables, no fixed calendar; coefficients are not re-estimated every round.
  • pe-microsim does not forecast. Every row here is boe-svar. How a score is built
Why publish a record this sparse

A handful of scores is not an accuracy headline; publishing from day one makes early failures part of the evidence.

Bank Rate comes closest to an accuracy gain, which does not survive the multiple-testing adjustment; it is not forecast here yet. Rounds follow data-edge refreshes.

How we score six rules, each enforced in code
  1. Archive firstThe round's JSON is committed before the outturn is published.
  2. Never editCI rejects any edit or deletion; a correction is a new round.
  3. No hindsightA round archived after the outturn's snapshot is never scored on it.
  4. Dated outturnsRead from a dated ONS snapshot; revisions are appended, never overwritten.
  5. Error and coverageSigned error of the median; coverage of the bands as archived.
  6. BenchmarksBeside a no-change forecast and any published path for that quarter.

Archive and freeze. A round is one JSON file per model under forecasts/rounds/<date>/, holding the median and the 68% and 90% bands for every quarter, the data edge (the last observation the model saw), and the hash of the artifact it was copied from. The forecast-archive workflow fails any pull request that edits, renames or deletes one, so the Git timestamp is the proof of when the claim was made.

No hindsight. score.py scores a period only when the round's archive date is on or before the date of the snapshot the outturn was read from; a later round that also covers the quarter stays in the archive but is never scored on it. The test is by date, not by the publisher's release time. The one round where that difference matters is the first, archived after the CPI release from a file committed before it; the archive says so.

Outturns. ingest_outturns.py converts the stored ONS series to the forecasts' units (GDP to year-on-year growth from the ABMI level; CPI and unemployment as published) and appends each value with its snapshot date. When the ONS revises a quarter a second value is appended; the scoreboard uses the newest, and every earlier score stays reproducible from outturns.json.

Errors and bands. Errors are the archived median minus the outturn, so a positive error means the forecast ran high; they are signed because a steady bias and random misses are different problems. Band coverage is scored on the bands exactly as archived, not on the re-calibrated bands drawn in the open-round charts.

Benchmarks. The minimum bar is a no-change forecast: the last outturn at the round's data edge, held flat. Where a stored quarterly path exists, the Bank of England's February 2026 MPR central projection, the OBR's March 2026 EFO and the Bank's April 2026 survey of external forecasters are shown beside it, from the committed official CSVs. Everything lands in scorecard.json, which is generated, never edited by hand.
