The first round was archived retroactively from a forecast file already committed to Git. That commit preceded the CPI outturn by about 16 hours, so the forecast was genuinely prior—but with thin lead time and weaker provenance than future rounds. Every later round is archived directly before the fact.

Rounds are append-only. Files under forecasts/rounds/ can be added but never edited or deleted, and a CI job fails any pull request that tries. Corrections create a new round; the superseded forecast remains scored.

The scoring inputs are downloadable too: outturns.json (versioned official outturns) and scorecard.json (the generated scores behind this page).

03 — how it is scored

Four numbers, not one.

A single accuracy headline hides the failures that matter, so each round is scored on four things:

01

Mean absolute error

Typical size of a miss, in percentage points.

02

Signed bias

Whether forecasts are persistently high or low rather than wrong in random directions.

03

Band coverage

Whether 68% and 90% ranges contain outturns as often as they claim.

04

Baseline comparison

Whether the model improves on random-walk, drift, and AR(1) forecasts.

Why outturns are versioned rather than overwritten

Official data is revised, sometimes substantially and sometimes years later. A track record that always scores against the latest vintage quietly rewrites its own history every time the ONS revises a quarter, and can flatter or punish a forecast that never changed.

So forecasts/outturns.json stores the vintage alongside every observation and appends revisions rather than replacing them. Each score records which vintage it was computed against, and can be reproduced exactly.

04 — what this is not

Limits worth stating plainly.

  • The record is short. A handful of rounds is not a track record, and will not be one for at least a year. Nothing on this page should be read as demonstrated forecasting skill until the scored count is well into double figures.
  • Three variables only. UK GDP growth and CPI inflation from the boe-svar rounds, and the unemployment rate from the Okun satellite round. Bank Rate, wage growth and borrowing are not forecast here yet.
  • The coefficients are not re-estimated each round. Between rounds only the conditioning data edge moves; the estimation sample is extended occasionally, not continuously. The 2026-07-21 round was estimated on the paper's 1992Q1–2023Q2 sample; the model was re-estimated on 1992Q1–2025Q1 in late July 2026, and each archived round records the sample it was estimated on.
  • There is no fixed calendar yet. A new round is archived when a forecast is issued, and each round is scored when its target data is released. Rounds so far follow data-edge refreshes; a regular cadence is the intended next step, and until it exists the round spacing is itself a discretionary choice.
