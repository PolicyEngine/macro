Emulator of the OBR macroeconometric model

OBR emulator

Scores second-round GDP effects of household-income reforms; behaviour checked for income tax and NICs only.

  • UK
  • Anchored, not validated
  • Hosted
  • Quarterly
Overview Methodology Validation Use it
Scores income tax and NICs changes; not a forecaster. Evidence → Tracks the October 2026 EFO by construction, so the fit shows no skill; weak when run without add-factors.

Good for

  • Income tax and NICs changes: the only reforms with checked behaviour; other household-income reforms run unchecked, and corporation tax is refused
  • GDP, consumption and investment, quarter by quarter, against the official path

Not for

  • Who gains and loses: PolicyEngine microsimulation
  • Long-run structural change: OG-UK
  • Where the economy is today: BoE SVAR
  • Bank Rate and corporation tax: the shocks run, but responses fail their benchmarks (limits)
  • Spending multipliers, capital spending, borrowing or prices (CPI does not respond to rate or oil shocks): limits

How far to trust it

Three checks, weakest included; each opens its evidence.

  • Anchored fit, real GDPReal GDP, 2025Q1–2027Q4: matched by design, so not skill0.44% MAPEby construction
  • Held add-factors, real GDP7 of 8 in band, but fitted on OBR-forecast quarters, so partly agreement by design0.35% MAPEweak
  • Free-running, real GDPThe emulator’s own dynamics, not OBR forecast error4.25% MAPEweak

Read the direction and scale, not single quarters. These are point estimates with no confidence bands, and several channels do not transmit (all limits).

Run it

The income-tax example as a reform: 1p on the basic rate. The hosted run uses the current five-year window, so its figures differ from the example’s 2025 start, and it takes over ten minutes.

Score a 1p rise in the UK basic rate of income tax with the OBR emulator and show the change in GDP each quarter.The hosted server calls score_reform with model="obr"; no install. Connect

pe-macro score --country uk \
  --reform '{"gov.hmrc.income_tax.rates.uk[0].rate":0.21}' \
  --model obr
from policyengine_macro import score_reform
result = score_reform("uk", {"gov.hmrc.income_tax.rates.uk[0].rate": 0.21}, "obr")

Inputs
A PolicyEngine microsimulation static costing, or a curated shock to one model variable
Outputs
GDP, consumption, investment: deviations from the anchored baseline
Horizon
Quarterly, typically 3-5 years
Data
October 2026 EFO baseline · October 2025 equation listing · vendored ONS snapshot
Uncertainty
Not comprehensive: read direction and scale, not single quarters
Access
Hosted MCP, CLI, Python
Runtime
About one to five minutes for a raw shock on the hosted server; over ten minutes for a translated reform on the default five-year window
Joins the microsimulation
The static costing enters as a household income add-factor (the join above)
Status
Anchored to the October 2026 EFO, not validated as a forecaster; predictive and policy checks weak
Cannot answer
Borrowing (not returned by the adapter), statutory incidence, capital spending, corporation tax through the bridge (limits)
