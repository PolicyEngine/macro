Scores fiscal changes; not a forecaster. Evidence →
Why

Tracks the March 2026 EFO by construction and is weak when run free: a scoring model for fiscal changes, not a forecaster.

Good for

  • Income tax and NICs changes: the one live household channel
  • GDP, consumption and investment, quarter by quarter, against the official path
  • Direct shocks to Bank Rate or the corporation tax rate

Not for

  • Who gains and loses: PolicyEngine microsimulation
  • Long-run structural change: OG-UK; where the economy is today: BoE SVAR
  • Spending multipliers, capital spending, borrowing, prices (CPI does not respond to rate or oil shocks): limits

What it returns

Four sustained shocks, applied from 2025Q1 (the first quarter of the OBR databank) and solved for 12 quarters; GDP is the change from the anchored baseline.

The microsimulation’s costing enters as lower household income, and consumption falls. The ratio builds up where the OBR’s fades. In real GDP, which obr_shock reports, the q12 ratio is 0.33× (why two bases). The run uses an earlier costing, £1.6bn a quarter (£6.46bn a year); at the current £6.55bn the model, linear in shock size, gives the same ratios and GDP about 1.4% larger.

By construction The shock enters the expenditure identity directly, so GDP rises by about the shock. That is accounting, not a behavioural multiplier (limits).

Only consumption and household and corporate interest flows respond. Investment, sterling, house prices and gilt yields have no interest-rate equation, so the fall is about 3–5× below the Bank of England’s published range (benchmark, limits).

A direct shock to the rate (obr_shock, TCPRO) through the cost of capital under the investment closure. There is no published OBR benchmark. The reform bridge refuses corporation tax, and the run is still far from its plateau after 12 quarters (limits).

With the microsimulation

How a reform reaches it: a static costing in, second-round effects out, the order the OBR scores in.

Reform → PolicyEngine microsimulationstatic costing → OBR emulatorhousehold income add-factor → GDP, consumption, investment

The costing enters as an add-factor on household disposable income (mechanics; every join in the suite).

Read the direction and scale, not single quarters. These are point estimates with no confidence bands, and several channels do not transmit (all limits).
