Transpile, solve, swap the closure

1

Transpile the published listing

The OBR publishes its equation listing as EViews source, which few can run. The transpiler rewrites every line (dlog(), @elem, lags like X(-1), error-correction terms) as an equivalent Python expression.

About this step
Translation
Growth rates
dlog(X)→log⁡Xt−log⁡Xt−1
Lags & elements
Xt−1, @elem → indexed array reads

What comes out

The published listing in open code, not a complete working copy of the OBR’s model.

  • All 372 equations parse; about 79 are skipped at solve time because their inputs are not published.
  • Some headline variables pass through at their published values rather than being computed.
  • Business investment uses an equation the OBR had commented out of its listing, reconstructed here.
2

Initialise from official data

History loads from the OBR's detailed forecast tables. The listing references 261 endogenous variables the outlook does not print; 199 have ONS series codes, supplied by a vendored 355-series ONS snapshot.

About this step
Data sources
Forecast tables
OBR detailed forecast tables, March 2026 EFO
ONS snapshot
355 vendored series; 199 of 261 unprinted variables matched

Why it matters

The snapshot ships inside the package, so there is no download step. It gives the circular fiscal and financial sub-blocks real balancing data rather than guessed starting values.

3

Solve by Gauss–Seidel iteration

The equations depend on each other, so each quarter is solved simultaneously, then the quarters in sequence.

About this step
Solver
Within quarter
cycle → update → repeat to convergence
Across quarters
sequential forward solve

Runtime

A twelve-quarter anchored solve takes roughly 30–40 seconds single-threaded; a reform run is two such solves.

4

Anchor to the published EFO

Add-factors, the residuals that reconcile each behavioural equation to data, are set so the baseline reproduces the published EFO. The OBR anchors the same way; these values are fitted here, not the OBR's own.

About this step
Anchoring
Add-factor window
fit 2024Q1–2025Q4, hold constant, project to 2027Q4
Scenario solves
12 quarters, 2025Q1–2027Q4
Anchored horizon
to 2031Q1 (fit on the validation tab)

The path every scenario is read against.

Forecasting with held add-factors

To forecast rather than score a one-off shock, the emulator fits add-factors over 2024Q1–2025Q4, holds them constant and projects to 2027Q4. Exogenous inputs are extrapolated (seasonal cash series at their trailing annual mean).

Horizon limits

How closely the anchor holds out to 2031Q1, and where unemployment stops being usable, is on the validation tab.

5

Swap the closure, shock, compare

In the OBR file GDP (GDPM) is an exogenous input and inventories (DINV) are the residual that closes the expenditure identity. The emulator swaps the two so output can respond.

About this step
Closure and shock
Closure swap
DINV equation removed and held at EFO; GDP identity added, GDPM endogenous
Policy effect
Xtshocked−Xtbaseline, identical structure

Closure swap

Holding DINV at its EFO path and adding a GDP identity makes GDPM endogenous: output responds to the policy instead of being fixed.

Shock against an identical baseline

Shocked and baseline paths share one structure, so their difference isolates the policy effect from the underlying forecast.

6

Score a costed reform through the microsimulation bridge

The join from a statutory reform to GDP: the microsimulation costs the reform, and the emulator computes the second round.

About this step
The bridge
Injection
HHDI_ADDFACTOR = −(annual costing) × 1000 ÷ 4, £m/qtr, held
Transmission
HHDI → RHHDI → CONS → GDPM
Command
pe-macro score --country uk --reform '{…}' --model obr

Static costing in, second-round effects out

PolicyEngine's microsimulation costs a statutory reform year by year: who pays and how much.

  • score_reform(country="uk", reform=..., model="obr") hands this model the bill.
  • Each year's costing enters as HHDI_ADDFACTOR, an add-factor on household disposable income.
  • It is minus the annual £bn, spread flat across four quarters (×1000 ÷ 4 → £m/qtr) and held.

What the emulator computes

  • Lower income moves consumption through the published consumption equation, and consumption moves GDP under the demand closure.
  • The second round runs quarter by quarter against the anchored baseline of step 4.
  • The anchoring add-factors are fixed in both runs, so they cancel in the difference.
  • The Overview’s worked example shows this for 1p on the basic rate.

Two bases for the ratio

  • The model reports GDP in chained volumes (real); the costing is in current prices.
  • GDP lost per pound of revenue is quoted in current prices, the basis of the OBR’s own multipliers.
  • The real-GDP ratio that obr_shock returns is smaller by the GDP deflator (obr-macroeconomic-model #63).

Where the bridge stops

Only household-borne costings enter; corporation tax and US reforms are refused (limits). For the other joins, see combine the models.
