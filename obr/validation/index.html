Free-running

Weak: 8 of 10 non-trivial lines in band

What the equations do with GDP made endogenous and no add-factors. The miss is the emulator’s, not OBR forecast error.

The free-running scorecard and the October 2026 re-anchoring

What the miss measures. Not OBR forecast error.

  • In the OBR's published model GDP (GDPM) is an exogenous input, so that model cannot miss GDP.
  • The gap measures this emulator's closure swap, which makes GDP endogenous, and inputs the OBR does not publish.
  • So reform deltas are scored against the anchored baseline, never the raw one.

Company profits, the worst line.

  • The miss does not trace to households' operating-surplus constant OSHH: its ONS anchor applies in the raw solve too.
  • Under the default closure profits miss by 12.88%. Under the published closure the investment deflator drifts in the solve and the miss reaches 22.47% (nominal GDP limit).
  • The line is regression-gated, not re-tuned: tuning it would be fitting to the answer.

Household taxes, held on their data path.

  • Households’ taxes on income and wealth (TYWHH) sum ONS components that are not seasonally adjusted against a seasonally adjusted total. The gap put a first-quarter dip into raw household income (−10.6% in 2025Q1, −12.5% in 2026Q1).
  • From model pin 0fe04c9 an identity residual closes the line, as the OBR’s own listing does: GDP 4.25% → 4.05%, consumption 7.24% → 6.92%, household income 4.30% → 3.08%, profits 12.32% → 11.66%. Since #74 (pin 0e44ec8): 4.24%, 7.23%, 3.10% and 12.88%.
  • The fall is the removal of a data-basis artefact, not better fit: the household-income score no longer tests that identity.

What it cannot be used for

Each limit opens to its evidence. Only income tax and NICs carry behaviour.

The spending multiplier is close to 1 by construction, against the OBR’s own published 0.6.

Why the multiplier is close to 1Spending lands straight in the GDP identity

The OBR’s 0.6 is for day-to-day public services and welfare spending. A spending shock lands straight in the GDP identity, so £1.25bn a quarter in returns about £1.27bn to £1.30bn of GDP a quarter (figures).

  • From model pin 0e44ec8 the shock is nominal-consistent by default (obr-macroeconomic-model #75): nominal spending moves by the same cash amount, so the government deflator is unchanged and borrowing rises by the cash cost, +£1,336m at q1 and +£1,419m at q12. Receipts have no equation in the listing and do not respond.
  • The real-only shock, the earlier default, spilled into the investment deflator and moved business investment by +£295m on impact; that artefact is gone (about −£2m).

Channels that do not transmit

VAT barely reaches GDP by default

A 1pp VAT rise is £2.2bn a quarter on VREC. Receipts and borrowing move one for one, but GDP barely moves, against the OBR’s published VAT multiplier of 0.35, fading.

Government investment does not transmit by default

Under the default closure CGIPS does not reach GDP, against the OBR’s published multiplier for capital spending of 1.0. An opt-in closure passes it through as an identity. Neither is a validated capital-spending multiplier.

  • Default. Total fixed investment (IF) moves exactly zero at any shock size: it has no live equation in the published listing, so the chain never reaches the GDP identity. The model warns on every such run.
  • Business investment (IBUSX) moves only as an offset. The listing makes it a residual (IBUS = IF − GGI − …, l.84), so higher CGIPS displaces it one for one in real terms.
  • £1.5bn a quarter for eight quarters from 2026Q1: the offset is −£1,408m at q4 and q8 (−£1,404m on average), linear to −£5,634m and −£5,633m for £6bn.
  • GDP falls by £3.8m at q4 and £7.3m at q8 for £1.5bn, the consumption that follows profits. Unemployment does not move.
  • The offset is accounting, not a multiplier, and must never be quoted as one.
  • Opt-in investment closure. investment_closure=True switches on the listing’s commented-out IF identity (l.86), so real government investment enters GDP one for one. A nominal £3bn a quarter for 12 quarters from 2025Q1 moves real GDP by +£3,075m at q1, +£2,901m at q4 and +£2,903m at q12.
  • That is identity passthrough at about 1.0 per real pound, which the result labels mechanical_passthrough: no taper, no crowding-out, and the nominal-GDP pin is off under this closure. The OBR’s 1.0 tapers to zero.
  • The default figures are measured at model pin 0e44ec8 under the nominal-GDP pin; every quarter of the baseline and shocked solves converges at every size.

Whole blocks do not respond to policy

This is about shock response, distinct from the baseline passthroughs.

  • In default runs the unemployment rate, employment, retail prices, exports and imports move exactly zero, not approximately zero, across every lever tested: government consumption, the household tax bridge, corporation tax, Bank Rate, exchange rate.
  • There is no Okun channel, no Phillips channel and no trade channel. A 6% sterling appreciation moves exports and imports not at all.
  • The second round is consumption and nothing else; under the investment closure it is business investment instead.
  • Pinned per block in tests/test_sign_conventions.py.
Passthrough channels

Exports, imports and CPI are exogenous here, held at the OBR value. They score 0.00% error because they are copied, not modelled. 10 of 21 scorecard lines are passthroughs, labelled as such.

Two household-income equations never fire

The profits → dividends → household-income channel is inert in every published figure.

  • The listing's only bare log() left-hand sides, log(HHTFA) and log(NDIVHH), never execute: inputs MAJGDP and CORP are absent from the databank.
  • A 5pp corporation-tax rise moves FYCPR by −£413m by quarter 12, −£191m on average over 12 quarters (results/site_figures.json), and NDIVHH by exactly zero.
  • Reviving the channel needs a CORP series.
Bank Rate: sign right, GDP response about 3–6× below the Bank of England’s range

A sustained +100bp lowers GDP (figures on the Overview); a 100bp cut raises it by a similar amount (about £134m to £519m).

  • The response runs through household spending alone. There is no monetary rule; unemployment, prices and trade do not move.
  • A 1pp rise held for four quarters lowers GDP by about 0.06% at peak, in the published closure and under the nominal-GDP pin (obr-macroeconomic-model #50).
  • The Bank of England’s published range for the same experiment is −0.2% to −0.35% (the benchmark). The model, the shock, the horizon and the monetary response all differ: why the gap.
  • The default holds the mortgage rate on its EFO path. The model’s opt-in monetary_conditioning=True moves it with Bank Rate at the listing’s own pass-through and gives −0.149% at peak, still below the range. It is a labelled overlay on run_reform, off by default and not exposed by obr_shock.
  • The benchmark dates from 1999. A sustained rise never turns within 12 quarters, and company profits move by an artefact of the nominal-GDP pin, so do not read profits from rate shocks.
  • Each figure is the difference between a baseline and a shocked solve under the default nominal-GDP pin; every quarter of both converges.

Results set by construction or simplified

Income tax: similar size to the OBR, opposite time profile

The household route is the one lever with behaviour in it. Its GDP effect per pound of revenue lands near the OBR’s published figure (worked example) but rises over the horizon where the OBR’s decays. The working paper calls the profile inverted.

Corporation-tax closure converges slowly

The published TCPRO → cost-of-capital → investment chain: a controlled scenario closure, not a calibrated supply block.

  • A sustained +5pp rise heads toward a ~£0.96bn/q plateau (figures to q12), but the root is slow (~0.958/quarter): a 12-quarter run captures only ~40% of the full effect, so every result reports its plateau_fraction.
  • The deviation has reached a steady state since the anchor add-factors moved to log space (August 2026).
  • The business-investment equation is reconstructed from a line the OBR publishes commented out; MSGVA, PIF and PIRHH are frozen against uncalibrated feedback.
  • The response size rests on allowance present values estimated from statute and the OBR's own gilt assumption, not invented constants. Full expensing makes it ~0.42× its former magnitude.
Approximated add-factors

Recent corrections are averaged and held flat. The OBR's judgemental, quarter-by-quarter add-factors are not reproduced.

No behavioural micro

Aggregate equations only. Distributional questions belong to PolicyEngine.

Hosted runs, closure and vintage

Anchored unemployment is the EFO’s, by construction

Anchored, unemployment (LFSUR) follows the EFO path: 5.07% at 2027Q4 and 4.17% at 2031Q1, the same as the EFO. It is not a forecast.

  • From model pin 0e44ec8 the forecast 16+ population follows the path the EFO’s own unemployment, employment and participation imply (obr-macroeconomic-model #74). Before, it was held flat at 56.4m, so unemployment drained to 1.0% by 2031Q1.
  • Run free, the unemployment rate misses the EFO by 0.62pp on average (step 3).
  • Fiscal and rate shocks still leave it exactly zero (channels that do not transmit).
Hosted results depend on the model pin and window

This repository pins the hosted service to 0e44ec8, on the October 2026 EFO. The server takes a new pin when it redeploys; every result records the one it ran at in provenance.source_revision.

  • At 0e44ec8 the nominal-GDP pin is the default (obr-macroeconomic-model #54–#58), the ended EU budget payments are held at zero (#65), the household-tax ratio is reported on both bases (#66), household taxes are held on their data path (#71), the two opt-in routes are labelled (#70), the forecast population follows the EFO (#74) and the spending shock is nominal-consistent (#75).
  • So obr_shock and score_reform(model="obr") results at this pin converge in every quarter and are scored around the anchored baseline, like the figures on these pages.
  • The default window differs from the worked example: score_reform scores five years, from 2026Q2 to 2031Q1; the example is a 12-quarter run from 2025Q1.
  • A hosted score_reform takes over ten minutes on the default window, mostly the five yearly microsimulation costings. Run it through start_job, or pass years=1 or 2 (macro #243).
Nominal GDP needs the pin to converge

The listing writes one identity twice (lines 337 and 688), to set the investment deflator (PIF) and nominal GDP (GDPMPS), so the published closure pins neither down.

  • PIF drifts, nominal GDP follows it, and company profits (FYCPR), a residual, follow nominal GDP (obr-macroeconomic-model #42). Under that closure no quarter converges.
  • The nominal-GDP pin restores the OBR’s own line 690. It is the default at this pin, the Overview figures use it, and with it every quarter converges.
  • The free-running scorecard on this page uses the pin: company profits miss by 12.88%, with 8 of 10 non-trivial lines in band, 9 of 11 counting one identity (scorecard at 0e44ec8, October 2026 EFO). The published closure gives 22.47% with 8 of 11 (obr-macroeconomic-model #52).
