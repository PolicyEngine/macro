The free-running scorecard and the March 2026 re-anchoring

What the miss measures. Not OBR forecast error.

  • In the OBR's published model GDP (GDPM) is an exogenous input, so that model cannot miss GDP.
  • The gap measures this emulator's closure swap, which makes GDP endogenous, and inputs the OBR does not publish.
  • So reform deltas are scored against the anchored baseline, never the raw one.

Company profits, the worst line.

  • The miss does not trace to households' operating-surplus constant OSHH: its ONS anchor applies in the raw solve too.
  • Built from its own identity on the loaded inputs, profits already miss by 23.9%. Those inputs mix EFO and ONS vintages, so that part has no behavioural error.
  • The rest comes from the investment deflator drifting in the solve (nominal GDP limit).
  • The line is regression-gated, not re-tuned: tuning it would be fitting to the answer.

Scope.

  • Two lines are not in the chart above: real household income 5.94%, RPI 1.71pp.
  • Every figure is the March 2026 vintage, after the upstream OSHH ONS anchor and the recovery of eight ONS series a failed download had dropped (355 series in all).

Vintage. Re-anchored from the November 2025 EFO to March 2026.

  • Every headline on this page is computed on the March 2026 baseline; only the outturn backtest above retains November 2025.
  • Reform effects are differences between structurally identical runs, so the re-anchoring leaves the static costing untouched.
  • Anchoring add-factors are fixed in baseline and shocked runs and cancel in the difference.
  • Second-round GDP is quoted as a ratio to revenue raised, not a per-quarter percentage, so it sits beside the OBR’s published multipliers (worked example).

What it cannot be used for

Each limit opens to its evidence. Only income tax and NICs carry behaviour.

The spending multiplier is close to 1 by construction, against the OBR’s own published 0.6.

Why the multiplier is close to 1Spending lands straight in the GDP identity

The OBR’s 0.6 is for day-to-day public services and welfare spending. A spending shock lands straight in the GDP identity, so £5bn in returns about £5bn of GDP (figures).

Channels that do not transmit

VAT barely reaches GDP

A 1pp VAT rise is £2.2bn a quarter on VREC. Receipts and borrowing move one for one.

  • GDP moves only 0.026 per pound raised after a year and 0.044 after three, against the OBR’s published 0.35.
  • CPI is exogenous in the OBR’s listing, so VAT cannot pass into prices. The tax lands on company profits, and only the withdrawals that follow profits reach households.
  • Only income tax and NICs transmit through household income.
  • Source: the emulator’s demo run of 15 Nov 2026, every quarter converged (obr-macroeconomic-model #67).
Government investment does not transmit

Capital spending should not be scored on this model. CGIPS fails to transmit in two separate ways, against the OBR’s published multiplier for capital spending of 1.0.

  • Total fixed investment (IF) moves exactly zero at any shock size: it has no live equation in the published listing, so the chain never reaches the GDP identity.
  • Business investment (IBUSX) moves, but only as an offset. The listing makes it a residual (IBUS = IF − GGI − …, l.84), so with IF fixed a rise in CGIPS (through GGI) displaces it by exactly the change in real government investment.
  • For a £1.5bn-a-quarter shock the offset is −£1,407m at q4 and −£1,348m at q12, scaling linearly to −£5,628m and −£5,390m for £6bn.
  • GDP falls slightly, by −£3.8m and −£10.8m for £1.5bn: the fall in consumption that follows profits. Unemployment does not move.
  • The offset is accounting, not a multiplier, and must never be quoted as one.
  • Measured at model pin 6fbbe06 under the nominal-GDP pin; every quarter of the baseline and shocked solves converges at every size. Source: OBR emulator CI run 43779573540, 16 Nov 2026.
Whole blocks do not respond to policy

This is about shock response, distinct from the baseline passthroughs.

  • The unemployment rate, employment, retail prices, exports and imports move exactly zero, not approximately zero, across every lever tested: government consumption, the household tax bridge, corporation tax, Bank Rate, exchange rate.
  • There is no Okun channel, no Phillips channel and no trade channel. A 6% sterling appreciation moves exports and imports not at all.
  • The second round is consumption and nothing else; under the investment closure it is business investment instead.
  • Pinned per block in tests/test_sign_conventions.py.
Passthrough channels

Exports, imports and CPI are exogenous here, held at the OBR value. They score 0.00% error because they are copied, not modelled. 10 of 21 scorecard lines are passthroughs, labelled as such.

Two household-income equations never fire

The profits → dividends → household-income channel is inert in every published figure.

  • The listing's only bare log() left-hand sides, log(HHTFA) and log(NDIVHH), never execute: inputs MAJGDP and CORP are absent from the databank.
  • A 5pp corporation-tax rise moves FYCPR by −£404m by quarter 12, −£186m on average over 12 quarters (results/site_figures.json), and NDIVHH by exactly zero.
  • Reviving the channel needs a CORP series.
Bank Rate: sign right, GDP response about 3–5× below the Bank of England’s range

A sustained +100bp lowers GDP (figures on the Overview); a 100bp cut raises it by a similar amount (about £134m to £541m).

  • The response runs through household spending alone. There is no monetary rule; unemployment, prices and trade do not move.
  • A 1pp rise held for four quarters lowers GDP by about 0.06% at peak, in the published closure and under the nominal-GDP pin (obr-macroeconomic-model #50).
  • The Bank of England’s published range for the same experiment is −0.2% to −0.35% (the benchmark).
  • Each figure is the difference between a baseline and a shocked solve under the default nominal-GDP pin; every quarter of both converges. Source: obr-macroeconomic-model at 6fbbe06, re-measured 15 Nov 2026.

Results set by construction or simplified

Income tax: similar size to the OBR, opposite time profile

The household route is the one lever with behaviour in it. Its GDP effect per pound of revenue lands near the OBR’s published figure (worked example) but rises over the horizon where the OBR’s decays. The working paper calls the profile inverted.

Corporation-tax closure converges slowly

The published TCPRO → cost-of-capital → investment chain: a controlled scenario closure, not a calibrated supply block.

  • A sustained +5pp rise heads toward a ~£0.95bn/q plateau (figures to q12), but the root is slow (~0.958/quarter): a 12-quarter run captures only ~40% of the full effect, so every result reports its plateau_fraction.
  • The deviation has reached a steady state since the anchor add-factors moved to log space (August 2026).
  • The business-investment equation is reconstructed from the OBR's truncated line; MSGVA, PIF and PIRHH are frozen against uncalibrated feedback.
  • The response size rests on allowance present values estimated from statute and the OBR's own gilt assumption, not invented constants. Full expensing makes it ~0.42× its former magnitude.
Approximated add-factors

Recent corrections are averaged and held flat. The OBR's judgemental, quarter-by-quarter add-factors are not reproduced.

No behavioural micro

Aggregate equations only. Distributional questions belong to PolicyEngine.

Hosted runs, closure and vintage

Hosted results depend on the model pin and window

This repository pins the hosted service to 6fbbe06. The server runs that pin from its next deploy; until then it still serves e4d7faa, under the published closure.

  • At 6fbbe06 the nominal-GDP pin is the default (obr-macroeconomic-model #54–#58), the ended EU budget payments are held at zero (#65) and the household-tax ratio is reported on both bases (#66).
  • So obr_shock and score_reform(model="obr") results at this pin converge in every quarter and are scored around the anchored baseline, like the figures on these pages.
  • The default window differs from the worked example: score_reform scores five years, from 2026Q2 to 2031Q1; the example is a 12-quarter run from 2025Q1.
  • A hosted score_reform takes over ten minutes on the default window, mostly the five yearly microsimulation costings. Run it through start_job, or pass years=1 or 2 (macro #243).
Nominal GDP needs the pin to converge

The listing writes one identity twice (lines 337 and 688), to set the investment deflator (PIF) and nominal GDP (GDPMPS), so the published closure pins neither down.

  • PIF drifts, nominal GDP follows it, and company profits (FYCPR), a residual, follow nominal GDP (obr-macroeconomic-model #42). Under that closure no quarter converges.
  • The nominal-GDP pin restores the OBR’s own line 690. It is the default at this pin, the Overview figures use it, and with it every quarter converges.
  • The free-running scorecard on this page has not yet been re-run under the pin: its figures, including the 82.77% profits error, still come from the published closure.
  • The pin’s own scorecard at 6fbbe06, re-measured on 15 Nov 2026 after later fixes including ending EU budget payments, gives 12.45% with 8 of 11 computed lines in band. The published closure gives 43.64% with 6 of 11 (obr-macroeconomic-model #52).
