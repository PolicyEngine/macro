About this step

Forecast versus outturn. Comparing one forecast vintage with another tests agreement, not accuracy.

  • The emulator tracks the three 2025 outturns to within 0.06pp.
  • It misses the strong 2026Q1 outturn by roughly a quarter point.
  • The November EFO it inherits misses 2026Q1 the same way.
  • So it is mostly a test of that November vintage.
  • The emulator's own contribution is the 0.02–0.13pp gap between the two model rows.
  • ONS quarterly estimates are themselves revised.

What the emulator does with GDP made endogenous and no add-factors.

The free-running scorecard and the March 2026 re-anchoring

The free-running scorecard. The free-running miss is not a measure of OBR forecast error.

  • In the OBR's published model GDP (GDPM) is an exogenous input.
  • So that model cannot miss GDP.
  • The gap measures this emulator's closure swap, which makes GDP endogenous.
  • It also measures inputs the OBR does not publish.
  • So reform deltas are scored against the anchored baseline, never the raw one.
  • Company profits, the worst line, do not trace to households' operating-surplus constant OSHH.
  • Its ONS anchor applies in the raw solve too.
  • Built from its own identity on the loaded inputs, profits already miss by 23.9%.
  • Those inputs mix EFO and ONS vintages; the miss has no behavioural error.
  • The rest comes from the investment deflator drifting in the solve (nominal GDP limit).
  • The line is regression-gated, not re-tuned: tuning it would be fitting to the answer.
  • Two lines are not in the chart above: real household income 5.94%, RPI 1.71pp.
  • Every figure here is the March 2026 vintage.
  • It follows the upstream OSHH ONS anchor and the recovery of eight ONS series.
  • A failed download had dropped those eight (355 series in all).

Vintage. Re-anchored from the November 2025 EFO to March 2026.

  • Every headline on this page is computed on that baseline.
  • Only the outturn backtest above retains November 2025.
  • Changing its forecast vintage would erase the historical forecast being tested.
  • Reform effects are differences between structurally identical runs.
  • So the re-anchoring leaves the static costing untouched.
  • Second-round GDP is quoted as a ratio to revenue raised (the worked example).
  • Not a per-quarter percentage, so it sits beside the OBR’s published multipliers (limits).
  • Reforms are scored around the anchored baseline.
  • Anchoring add-factors are fixed in baseline and shocked runs and cancel in the difference.

What it cannot be used for

The spending multiplier is close to 1 by construction, against the OBR’s own published 0.6 for day-to-day public services and welfare spending. A spending shock lands straight in the GDP identity, so £5bn in returns about £5bn of GDP (figures).

Channels that do not transmit

VAT barely reaches GDP

A 1pp VAT rise is £2.2bn a quarter on VREC.

  • Receipts and borrowing move one for one.
  • GDP moves only 0.026 per pound raised after a year, 0.044 after three.
  • The OBR’s published figure is 0.35.
  • CPI is exogenous in the OBR’s listing, so VAT cannot pass into prices.
  • The tax lands on company profits.
  • Only the withdrawals that follow profits reach households.
  • Only income tax and NICs transmit through household income.
  • Source: the emulator’s demo run of 15 Nov 2026, every quarter converged (obr-macroeconomic-model #67).
Government investment does not transmit

CGIPS does not transmit, in two separate ways.

  • Total fixed investment (IF) moves exactly zero at any shock size.
  • IF has no live equation in the published listing.
  • So the chain never reaches the GDP identity.
  • Business investment (IBUSX) does move, but only as an offset.
  • The listing makes it a residual (IBUS = IF − GGI − …, l.84).
  • With IF fixed, a rise in CGIPS (through GGI) displaces it.
  • The displacement is exactly the change in real government investment.
  • For a £1.5bn-a-quarter shock: −£1,407m at q4 and −£1,348m at q12.
  • That scales linearly to −£5,628m and −£5,390m for £6bn.
  • GDP falls slightly: −£3.8m and −£10.8m for £1.5bn.
  • That equals the fall in consumption that follows profits; unemployment does not move.
  • Measured at model pin 6fbbe06 under the nominal-GDP pin.
  • Every quarter of the baseline and shocked solves converges at every size.
  • Source: OBR emulator CI run 43779573540, 16 Nov 2026.
  • That offset is accounting, not a multiplier, and must never be quoted as one.
  • The OBR’s published multiplier for capital spending is 1.0.
  • Capital spending should not be scored on this model.
Whole blocks do not respond to policy

Distinct from the baseline passthroughs above: this is about shock response.

  • Levers tested: government consumption, the household tax bridge, corporation tax, Bank Rate, exchange rate.
  • The unemployment rate, employment, retail prices, exports and imports do not respond.
  • Across every lever they move exactly zero, not approximately zero.
  • There is no Okun channel, no Phillips channel and no trade channel.
  • A 6% sterling appreciation moves exports and imports not at all.
  • The second round is consumption and nothing else.
  • Under the investment closure it is business investment instead.
  • This is pinned per block in tests/test_sign_conventions.py.
Passthrough channels

Exports, imports and CPI are exogenous here, held at the OBR value. They score 0.00% error because they are copied from the OBR, not modelled — 10 of 21 scorecard lines are passthroughs, labelled as such.

Two household-income equations never fire

The listing's only bare log() left-hand sides never execute.

  • log(HHTFA) and log(NDIVHH): inputs MAJGDP and CORP are absent from the databank.
  • So the profits → dividends → household-income channel is inert.
  • A 5pp corporation-tax rise moves FYCPR by −£404m by quarter 12.
  • That is −£186m on average over 12 quarters (results/site_figures.json).
  • The same rise moves NDIVHH by exactly zero.
  • Reviving the channel needs a CORP series.
  • Every published figure has the channel inert.
Bank Rate: sign right, GDP response about 3–5× below the Bank of England’s range

A sustained +100bp lowers GDP (figures on the Overview).

  • A 100bp cut raises it by a similar amount (about £134m to £541m).
  • Each figure is the difference between a baseline and a shocked solve.
  • Both use the default nominal-GDP pin, and every quarter of both converges.
  • Source: obr-macroeconomic-model at 6fbbe06; re-measured 15 Nov 2026.
  • The response runs through household spending alone.
  • There is no monetary rule; unemployment, prices and trade do not move.
  • A 1pp rise held for four quarters lowers GDP by about 0.06% at peak.
  • That holds in the published closure and under the nominal-GDP pin (obr-macroeconomic-model #50).
  • That is below the Bank of England’s published −0.2% to −0.35%.
  • Those figures are for the same experiment (the benchmark).

Results set by construction or simplified

Income tax: similar size to the OBR, opposite time profile

The household route is the one lever with behaviour in it. Its GDP effect as a ratio to revenue lands near the OBR’s published figure (worked example), but it rises over the horizon where the OBR’s decays — the working paper calls the profile inverted.

Corporation-tax closure converges slowly

The published TCPRO → cost-of-capital → investment chain.

  • The business-investment equation is reconstructed from the OBR's truncated line.
  • MSGVA, PIF and PIRHH are frozen against uncalibrated feedback.
  • The anchor add-factors moved to log space (August 2026).
  • Since then the deviation reaches a steady state.
  • A sustained +5pp rise heads toward a ~£0.95bn/q plateau (figures to q12).
  • But the root is slow (~0.958/quarter).
  • So a 12-quarter run captures only ~40% of the full effect.
  • For that reason every result reports its plateau_fraction.
  • The response size now rests on allowance present values.
  • They are estimated from statute and the OBR's own gilt assumption, not invented constants.
  • Full expensing makes it ~0.42× its former magnitude.
  • A controlled scenario closure, not a calibrated supply block.
Approximated add-factors

Recent corrections are averaged and held flat. The OBR's judgemental, quarter-by-quarter add-factors are not reproduced.

No behavioural micro

Aggregate equations only. Distributional questions belong to PolicyEngine.

Hosted runs, closure and vintage

Hosted results depend on the model pin and window

This repository pins the hosted service to 6fbbe06.

  • The server runs that pin from its next deploy.
  • Until then it still serves e4d7faa, under the published closure.
  • The nominal-GDP pin is the default (obr-macroeconomic-model #54–#58).
  • The ended EU budget payments are held at zero (#65).
  • The household-tax ratio is reported on both bases (#66).
  • So obr_shock and score_reform(model="obr") results at this pin converge in every quarter.
  • They are scored around the anchored baseline, like the figures on these pages.
  • The default window still differs from the worked example.
  • score_reform scores five years, from 2026Q2 to 2031Q1.
  • The example is a 12-quarter run from 2025Q1.
  • A hosted score_reform takes over ten minutes on the default window.
  • Most of that is the five yearly microsimulation costings.
  • Run it through start_job, or pass years=1 or 2 (macro #243).
Nominal GDP needs the pin to converge

The listing writes one identity twice (lines 337 and 688).

  • It sets the investment deflator (PIF) and nominal GDP (GDPMPS).
  • So the published closure pins neither down.
  • PIF drifts and nominal GDP follows it.
  • Company profits (FYCPR), a residual, follow nominal GDP (obr-macroeconomic-model #42).
  • Under that closure no quarter converges.
  • The nominal-GDP pin restores the OBR’s own line 690.
  • It is the default at this pin, and the Overview figures use it.
  • With the pin, every quarter converges.
  • The free-running scorecard on this page has not yet been re-run under the pin.
  • Its figures, including the 82.77% profits error, still come from the published closure.
  • The pin’s own scorecard at 6fbbe06 was re-measured on 15 Nov 2026.
  • That followed later fixes, including ending EU budget payments.
  • It gives 12.45% with 8 of 11 computed lines in band.
  • The published closure gives 43.64% with 6 of 11 (obr-macroeconomic-model #52).
Vintage

October 2025 equation listing, March 2026 EFO, current-vintage ONS series. Where the ONS has revised history the identities don't close exactly; that slack lands in the add-factors.
