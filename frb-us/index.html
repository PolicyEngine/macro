FRB/US

Traces how a US funds-rate or fiscal shock moves output, unemployment and inflation, quarter by quarter.

Overview Methodology Validation Use it
Solver matches pyfrbus; economics not tested. Evidence →
Why

Matches the Fed’s pyfrbus on one 100bp funds-rate shock: a solver check, not a test of the economics. Its baseline equals the Board’s by construction, and that baseline misses core inflation.

Good for

  • Monetary and fiscal shocks through the Board’s own equations
  • Quarterly paths against the Board’s April 2026 baseline
  • Wage and hours paths for the incidence bridge (experimental)

Not for

  • PolicyEngine reforms: score_reform refuses it (limits)
  • Forward guidance: no model-consistent expectations in the hosted tools
  • Anything about the UK: OBR emulator

What it returns

One command: a 100bp funds-rate tightening in 2026Q1, under the default inertial Taylor rule.

Shock applied+1.0ppfunds-rate rule error, 2026Q1
2027 wage bill−0.65%compensation per hour plus hours
pe-macro frbus-shock --var rffintay_aerr --shock 1.0

It returns the funds rate, real GDP, unemployment and core inflation, quarter by quarter. See the GDP, unemployment and inflation response, set against the Board’s documented simulation properties, with the fiscal multipliers.

With the microsimulation

Its labour-market path tells the microsimulation who bears a shock.

FRB/USwages · hours → PolicyEngine microsimulationUS employment income → Who bears it

frbus_shock_incidence scales US employment income with the wage and hours deviations, so federal and state taxes, SNAP and the EITC show who bears it (every join in the suite).

Uniform scaling is an experimental illustration: real incidence concentrates in job losers.
