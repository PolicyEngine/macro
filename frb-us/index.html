FRB/US

Traces how a US funds-rate or fiscal shock moves output, unemployment and inflation, quarter by quarter.

  • US
  • Replicates the Fed’s solver
  • Hosted
  • Quarterly
Overview Methodology Validation Use it
Solver matches the Fed’s code; economic checks weak. Evidence → Matches the Fed’s pyfrbus on one 100bp funds-rate shock: a solver check; the economic checks on the Validation tab are partial to weak. Its baseline equals the Board’s by construction, and that baseline misses both GDP growth and core PCE in its first projected quarter (2026Q3).

Good for

  • Monetary and fiscal shocks through the Board’s own equations
  • Quarterly paths against the Board’s October 2026 baseline
  • Wage and hours paths for the incidence bridge (experimental)

Not for

  • PolicyEngine reforms: score_reform refuses it (limits)
  • Forward guidance: no model-consistent expectations in the hosted tools
  • Anything about the UK: OBR emulator

A worked example

The Fed raises the funds rate by one percentage point for one quarter (2026Q1), under the default inertial Taylor rule.

Real GDP, trough−0.56%2027Q4, against baseline
Unemployment, peak+0.28pp2028Q1
Core PCE inflation, trough−0.034ppquarter 8

Real GDP falls 0.56% at its trough and unemployment rises 0.28pp at its peak, while core PCE falls 0.034pp at its trough; the Board’s own run of the current model gives −0.0345pp. See the responses against the Board’s own simulation.

How far to trust it

Three checks, weakest included; each opens its evidence.

  • Solver, 100bp funds-rate shockFed’s pyfrbus 1.0.0 and 1.1.1; gate 1e−61.4e−8 and 1.8e−8pass
  • 100bp shock, size of responsesBoard’s published run; core PCE and output gap matchunemployment +0.28 vs +0.219pppartial
  • Inflation effect of the 2022–23 hikesFRBSF Economic Letter 2025-03, about 3pp (model economics, not implementation)−0.12ppfail

Trust the solver, not the economics. The match shows the Board’s equations are solved correctly, not that they are right (all limits).
