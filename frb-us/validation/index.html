How it is gated, and the uncertainty engine

Uncertainty engine. Seeded stochastic simulation jointly resamples the official residual vector, preserving contemporaneous dependence; model and LONGBASE packages carry SHA-256 provenance gates. Published probability results still need a reviewed simulation design, and model-consistent expectations are not yet available through the hosted tools.

CI does more than re-check a committed CSV.

  • A dedicated job re-runs the Board's pyfrbus from source.
  • CI gates against that fresh solution.
  • A weekly run repeats the suite across Python 3.10–3.12.
  • The multiplier comparisons are refreshed manually.
  • Full tables are in VALIDATION.md and the working paper.

This is an independent implementation built from the Federal Reserve Board's published model equations and data. It is not produced, maintained, or endorsed by the Federal Reserve, and its results should not be presented as official Federal Reserve estimates.

About this step

The Fed-vs-Fed tile frames the other three.

  • The Board's two releases ship a byte-identical model.xml and LONGBASE.
  • They differ only in how Newton reuses its LU factorisation.
  • Yet they disagree by as much as this implementation disagrees with either.
  • Agreement sits at the reference's own numerical noise, not at a chosen tolerance.
  • All three residuals concentrate in the same near-zero expectational-gap series (wpsn, zgap05).

About this step

Multipliers. Read these as plausibility bands, not as a test passed.

  • CBO ranges marked “converted” come from Whalen and Reichling.
  • The source is CBO Working Paper 2015-02, Table 2.
  • It gives the quarterly response to a one-quarter $1 impulse.
  • We cumulate it into this page’s calendar-year ratio for a sustained shock.
  • The conversion is ours, not CBO’s.
  • The tax-cut ranges are from CBO (Feb 2015, Table 3).
  • That source assumes the Fed did not tighten.
  • Coenen et al. (AEJ: Macroeconomics 2012) values are read from their charts (±0.1).
  • They cover the 2009–10 FRB-US, whose expectations mode they do not state.
  • Ramey’s survey range pools estimates across horizons and monetary regimes.
  • Sources and the conversion: us-frb-model docs/benchmarks.md.

Known limitations

Known limits of the FRB/US implementation
LimitDetail
The baseline misses core inflation Against 2026 outturns the April 2026 LONGBASE tracks unemployment and the funds rate within 0.2pp in 2026Q1 and Q2. It tracks GDP growth within 0.3pp in 2026Q1 but overshoots it by 0.9pp in 2026Q2, and it misses core PCE inflation by −1.65pp (2.8% vs a 4.4% annualized outturn, 2026Q1; −0.65pp in 2026Q2). A weekly outturn backtest reports this rather than gating it, because the baseline is the Board’s. LONGBASE is an illustrative tracking baseline, not an official Fed forecast.
VAR expectations only The hosted tools run VAR expectations only, so forward guidance is out of scope here. The package itself now supports opt-in model-consistent expectations (Frbus(path, mce=...)), gated against the Board’s pyfrbus at under 1e-6, for use from Python.
No mcontrol Trajectory targeting is out of scope. stochsim_bands() does produce seeded bootstrap bands, but the hosted frbus_shock returns a single deterministic path.
Hosted, but only as raw shocks One lever, one shock size, a policy rule and a headline set of responses — not the full 284-variable model. Anything more means installing the package.
No PolicyEngine reform bridge Reforms enter as shocks, not statute: score_reform refuses model="frbus", so US reform scoring stops at PolicyEngine microsimulation's static answer.
A small inflation response A 100bp shock moves core PCE inflation by −0.034pp at the trough, as in the Board’s own simulation of the current model (worked shock). Almost all of it runs through the VAR-expectations equations. A scenario whose conclusion depends on inflation should say so (us-frb-model #15).
Little disinflation from the 2022–23 hikes FRB/US (VAR), as the Board publishes it, attributes about 0.1pp of the 2022–24 fall in core PCE inflation to the hikes (mean −0.12pp against never tightening, −0.11pp against following the rule from 2024). Empirical high-frequency estimates put it at about 3pp (FRBSF Economic Letter 2025-03). This follows from the small inflation response above (episode results, us-frb-model #14).
Vintage Later Board vintages of model.xml and LONGBASE are not adopted (the one in use is on the model card).
