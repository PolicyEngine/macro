Matching the Fed’s baseline does not make the baseline right: against the 2026Q3 outturn LONGBASE misses GDP growth and core PCE inflation (limits).

How it is gated, and the uncertainty engine

Uncertainty engine. Seeded stochastic simulation jointly resamples the official residual vector, preserving contemporaneous dependence; model and LONGBASE packages carry SHA-256 provenance gates. Published probability results still need a reviewed simulation design, and model-consistent expectations are not yet available through the hosted tools.

  • A dedicated CI job re-runs the Board's pyfrbus from source and gates against that fresh solution, not a committed CSV.
  • A weekly run repeats the suite on Python 3.11 and 3.12.
  • The multipliers are re-gated weekly; the published comparators and this table are refreshed by hand.
  • Full tables: VALIDATION.md and the working paper (the paper is on the April 2026 vintage).

An independent implementation of the Federal Reserve Board's published model equations and data. It is not produced, maintained or endorsed by the Federal Reserve; do not present its results as official Federal Reserve estimates.

The Fed-vs-Fed comparison frames the others.

  • The Board's two releases ship a byte-identical model.xml and LONGBASE, and differ only in how Newton reuses its LU factorisation.
  • Yet they disagree by as much as this implementation disagrees with either, so agreement sits at the reference's own numerical noise, not at a chosen tolerance.
  • All three residuals concentrate in the same series: the wage level wpsn in absolute terms, and near-zero gap terms (dpadj) in relative terms.

Known limitations

The baseline misses core inflation

Against the first outturn since its data edge, 2026Q3, the October 2026 LONGBASE tracks unemployment within 0.1pp and the funds rate within 0.2pp, undershoots GDP growth by 1.23pp (2.1% vs a 3.4% annualized outturn) and overshoots core PCE inflation by 0.80pp (3.3% vs 2.5%). The April 2026 vintage missed 2026Q1 core PCE by −1.65pp (April record). A weekly outturn backtest reports this rather than gating it, because the baseline is the Board’s. LONGBASE is an illustrative tracking baseline, not an official Fed forecast.

VAR expectations only

The hosted tools run VAR expectations only, so forward guidance is out of scope here. The package itself now supports opt-in model-consistent expectations (Frbus(path, mce=...)), gated against the Board’s pyfrbus at under 1e-6, for use from Python.

No mcontrol

Trajectory targeting is out of scope. stochsim_bands() does produce seeded bootstrap bands, but the hosted frbus_shock returns a single deterministic path.

Hosted, but only as raw shocks

One lever, one shock size, a policy rule and a headline set of responses — not the full 284-variable model. Anything more means installing the package.

No PolicyEngine reform bridge

Reforms enter as shocks, not statute: score_reform refuses model="frbus", so US reform scoring stops at PolicyEngine microsimulation's static answer.

A small inflation response

A 100bp shock moves core PCE inflation by −0.034pp at the trough, as in the Board’s own simulation of the current model (worked shock). Almost all of it runs through the VAR-expectations equations. A scenario whose conclusion depends on inflation should say so (us-frb-model #15).

Little disinflation from the 2022–23 hikes

FRB/US (VAR), as the Board publishes it, attributes about 0.1pp of the 2022–24 fall in core PCE inflation to the hikes (mean −0.12pp against never tightening, −0.11pp against following the rule from 2024). Empirical high-frequency estimates put it at about 3pp (FRBSF Economic Letter 2025-03). This follows from the small inflation response above (episode results, us-frb-model #14).

Vintage

New Board vintages of model.xml and LONGBASE are adopted by hand, not automatically; the one in use, October 2026, is on the model card. Its history runs through 2026Q2 while the simulation window still starts in 2026Q1.
