methodology

Rules, entities, and a dependency graph.

Every quantity in the system — an allowance, a taper, a means test — is a variable with a formula and a set of dated parameters. Asking for one variable resolves the graph beneath it, so a household net-income figure is computed from the statutory chain rather than approximated by a schedule. Variables are attached to entities, and the entity structure differs by country: the UK has three (person, benefit unit, household), the US six (person, tax unit, SPM unit, family, marital unit, household), because US programmes are assessed on differently drawn units.

A reform re-dates a parameter and re-resolves the same graph, which is why the household and population APIs take reforms in the identical format. Each release pins the country-model and dataset versions it certifies, so a published estimate can be traced to the exact bundle that produced it.

data enhancement

Survey microdata, enhanced.

Household calculations need no data at all — the inputs are the ones you type. Population results run the same rule graph over a representative survey dataset: for the UK the enhanced Family Resources Survey (currently the enhanced_frs_2023_24 vintage), for the US the Current Population Survey. The UK enhancement imputes under-reported incomes and reweights the survey so that aggregates better match administrative totals, and the resulting microdata is distributed through a gated Hugging Face repository (policyengine/policyengine-uk-data).

Because population estimates rest on survey data, they inherit sampling error and imputation and ageing assumptions in a way a household calculation does not — the consequences are set out on the validation page. Within the suite, this same microdata is what the OLG model consumes to estimate its tax functions, and the static costings it produces are what the OBR emulator takes as the direct effect.
