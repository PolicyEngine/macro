01 — what it is

Statute as code, at household resolution.

The package encodes UK and US tax-and-benefit rules as parameterised statute and computes every variable for a household you describe — UK: income tax, National Insurance, Universal Credit, Child Benefit, Pension Credit; US: federal and state income tax, payroll tax, SNAP, TANF, EITC, CTC, SSI and Social Security. A reform is a flat dict of parameter path → new value, identical at household and population level.

The limit is structural: it cannot answer GDP, inflation, interest rates or macro feedback. Its costings are static, and the macro members of the suite exist to supply what it cannot see.

The four packages underneath

policyengine-core (the simulation framework, forked from OpenFisca), policyengine-uk and policyengine-us (the statutory rules per country), and policyengine — the analyst-facing package covered here, adding dataset management, reforms, structured outputs and version-pinned release bundles for reproducibility. See also the code, the methodology, and how far to trust it.

with the microsimulation

Every bridge in the suite starts or ends here.

→Connection. This is the microsimulation: the static-costing input to the OBR emulator, the incidence engine for the SVAR, FRB/US, and HANK bridges, and both ends of the OLG two-way loop.
→Worked example. 1p on the basic rate: £6.46bn direct yield in 2026 with decile-by-decile incidence, before the macro feedback is added — the full chain on score a reform.
→Validation. Checked against implemented legislation, rule by rule; household maths exact, population estimates add survey error.
→Results. Household and population reform impacts from any connected client or at policyengine.org.
