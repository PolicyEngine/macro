Executable check
2026 within ±5%, skipped without microdata
  • Population costings are survey-weighted estimates; the pipeline produces no interval (limits).
  • HMRC’s June 2025 ready reckoner is post-behavioural on administrative SPI data. Ours is static and survey-based, so this is a benchmark, not a validation.
  • A static costing below a post-behavioural one runs against intuition. The paper’s leading explanation is the data basis: administrative data capture the top of the income distribution more completely than a survey. Nothing yet decomposes the gap.
  • The reckoner stops at 2028–29, so the 2030 figure has no counterpart.
  • Every figure comes from hosted runs under policyengine 6.11.1; job ids are in papers/pe-microsim/figures/basic_rate_costing.json. Under policyengine 4.x they were £6.46bn and £7.38bn.
  • The executable check is test_population_impact_uk_real_basic_rate in integration/tests/test_population.py.

Limits

What the model leaves out, and the data and versions it runs on.

Known limits of the tax-benefit microsimulation
LimitDetail
Static, no general equilibrium Prices, wages and output do not move; behavioural responses are optional and post-hoc.
UK data vintage enhanced_frs_2023_24; upstream's FRS 2024-25 vintage is not adopted here.
Survey-based aggregates Population estimates carry sampling, imputation and ageing error, and no interval is computed for them. HMRC's costings use administrative Survey of Personal Incomes data and embed taxable-income elasticities, so a few hundred million of difference on a broad-based rate change is expected.
Gated UK microdata Population runs need a HUGGING_FACE_TOKEN with access. Household calculations are unaffected.
Statute-vintage divergence with OLG This package tracks the latest release; the OLG member is pinned to policyengine-uk==2.88.0, so baseline statute can disagree — a declared inconsistency.
