Models are in the hub order, not ranked. Each verdict starts with one of six words: plausible, off, questionable, unresolved, not tested or no instrument; some add a qualifier (such as “off, sign right”), and each carries its reason and the open issue. These words grade one experiment; each model’s overall standing uses the status vocabulary on its model card. Results file version 2026-11-06.1; how it is checked and regenerated.

Benchmark range

  • Bank of England, 1pp rise held one year (UK). Output −0.2% to −0.35% after about a year; inflation −0.2 to −0.4pp a year or so after that. 1pp × 4 quarters = 4 pp-quarters, so 0.05–0.09% of GDP per pp-quarter. Bank of England, The transmission mechanism of monetary policy, Quarterly Bulletin 1999 Q2
  • Fed FRB/US, 100bp funds-rate shock (VAR expectations) (US). Output gap trough −0.4pp (FEDS Note 2014); about −0.53pp at quarter 6 (price–wage note 2013, Fig. 3, read off the chart); inflation about −0.08pp. Brayton, Laubach and Reifschneider, FEDS Note, 3 Apr 2014; FRB/US price–wage sector note, Aug 2013, p.16 (quoted in us-frb-model VALIDATION.md, Test 3)

GDP per pp-quarter divides the peak GDP effect by the rate path's size to the peak. HANK's figure uses the realised real-rate path; the other rows use the nominal policy rate, which is close to the real rate in FRB/US because its inflation barely moves. On HANK's rstar path (10 pp-quarters at ρ = 0.9) the figure would be about 0.04%, so HANK's 0.16 and FRB/US's 0.15 are not like-for-like.

HANK reports inflation as a quarterly rate; this page multiplies it by 4 to give an annual rate.
