Pick a question

Models are in the hub order, not ranked. The sourced benchmark sits in the first row; each model row gives its peak effects and a verdict, and Details holds the run, full units and caveats.

How to read the verdicts

Each verdict starts with one of six words: plausible, off, questionable, unresolved, not tested or no instrument; some add a qualifier (such as “off, sign right”), and each carries its reason and the open issue. These words grade one experiment; each model’s overall standing uses the status vocabulary on its model card. Results file version 2026-11-19.1; how it is checked and regenerated.

Why the two UK rows sit either side of the Bank of England's range

The OBR emulator is 3–6× below the range and the BoE SVAR is above it. The three are different models answering different experiments.

  • Benchmark. The Bank's 1999 illustration: Bank Rate 1pp higher for one year, then back. Output is −0.2% to −0.35% lower after about a year. It is one published range from 1999, not a current Bank estimate.
  • Model, OBR emulator. The OBR's published equations. Bank Rate reaches consumption and household interest payments, and nothing else. The mortgage rate, gilt yields, sterling and house prices are inputs with no equation; investment, trade, prices and unemployment do not respond. A small answer is the expected result of the missing channels.
  • Model, BoE SVAR. A statistical model: our replication of the Bank's own structural vector autoregression (Macro Technical Paper No. 3), estimated on 1992Q1–2025Q1. It has no named channels. Its answer is how GDP moved after past policy surprises.
  • Shock. The OBR row imposes the benchmark's own rate path (+1pp for four quarters) with everything else held. The SVAR row is a one-standard-deviation policy surprise identified by sign restrictions, scaled linearly to 100bp. A surprise and an announced path are different experiments.
  • Horizon. The benchmark and the OBR row are read at about a year (the OBR row peaks at q4). The SVAR row is read at h = 8 and is still building: its rate path has a unit root (a shock never fully dies out), so neither Bank Rate nor GDP returns within five years.
  • Monetary response. In the benchmark the rate returns after a year by assumption. The OBR emulator has no policy rule and no price response, so nothing feeds back to the rate. In the SVAR the Bank's usual reaction is part of the estimated dynamics and the rate stays high, so more tightening accumulates: per pp-quarter the gap narrows from 4.6–8× to 2–4×.
  • What narrows the gap, OBR emulator. Moving the mortgage rate with Bank Rate at the listing's own 0.9 pass-through (the opt-in monetary_conditioning) gives −0.149% at the peak, still below the range. Interest received has no equation, so neither figure is a bound on the full effect. (obr-macroeconomic-model docs/monetary_conditioning.md)
  • What narrows the gap, BoE SVAR. Part of it is the replication, not the method. For a surprise of about 10bp on impact, the committed replication run gives a GDP trough of about −0.16% where the Bank's paper shows about −0.07% (approximate read-offs from its figures). The row above uses the fast test fixture, where the same surprise is 25bp on impact; the effect per basis point is the same. Scaled to 100bp the same way, the paper's own figure is also above the 1999 range. Open. (boe-var-model results/sense_check.md at 17f5d82)

Three of five models answer. Off: OBR emulator; plausible: BoE SVAR and FRB/US. US HANK and DEFINE-UK have no instrument for it.
