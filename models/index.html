Three questions, three routes.

What would a reform cost — and what would it do to the economy?score a reform

The scoring pipeline chains two members: PolicyEngine's microsimulation produces the static costing from the actual tax-benefit rules, then the OBR emulator feeds it through the macro model for the second-round effects — the OBR's own "static costing in, second-round effects out" workflow.

Reform → pe-microsim → obr-macro → Scored result
→Worked example: 1p on the basic rate — £6.46bn (2026) → £7.38bn (2030), GDP −0.02% on impact building to −0.06%
→Static costing sits within HMRC's published ready-reckoner range
→Hosted — MCP tools or one CLI command

The score_reform MCP tool and the pe-macro score CLI take the same flat reform dict and a scoring model: microsim (static costing), obr (with macro feedback), og (long-run steady state; local-only), or og+microsim (dynamic).

pe-macro score --country uk --reform '{"gov.hmrc.income_tax.rates.uk[0].rate":0.21}' --model obr

Read the caveats before the headline: the direct costing inherits survey-microdata uncertainty, and the emulator's impact multiplier is ~1 by construction vs the OBR's published 0.6 — treat macro feedback as sign and order of magnitude. Step-by-step mechanics live on the microsimulation (the direct effect) and OBR emulator (the second round) pages; setup on the Use page.

Taxes and benefits for a household or populationUK + US · PolicyEngine

The microsimulation runs the actual tax-benefit rules as code over enhanced survey microdata (Enhanced FRS for the UK, CPS for the US), so it answers at both levels: one household’s net income under a reform, or the population-wide revenue and distributional effect.

→Hosted — MCP tools and a CLI, no account needed
→Household answers are exact rule arithmetic; population answers inherit survey-data error
→Static by design: no behavioural or macro feedback (the macro members add that)

Open PolicyEngine microsim →

A monetary or fiscal shock in the US over coming quartersUS · frb-us

The Federal Reserve’s FRB/US model — 284 behavioural equations parsed from the Board’s own model file — is solved by damped Newton around the April 2026 LONGBASE baseline; you pick the lever and the policy rule, and read the deviation path quarter by quarter.

→Levers: federal funds rate, government spending, transfers, taxes — each in its own units
→Replicates the Board’s dynamics to machine precision (residuals ~1e-8 or better)
→VAR expectations only; a conditioning baseline, not a forecast

Open FRB/US →

How US shock responses differ across the household wealth distributionUS · us-hank

A two-asset heterogeneous-agent New Keynesian model (an ABRS 2021 replication) solves the same monetary, fiscal or productivity shock for the whole distribution of households, then reports consumption responses by liquid-wealth quartile and the hand-to-mouth share.

→MPCs by wealth quartile alongside the aggregate response
→Warm shock in seconds via a cached sequence-space Jacobian
→Stylized replication — not a forecaster, no reform scoring

Open US HANK →

A selected UK fiscal or macro scenario over 3–5 yearsUK · obr-macro

A transpiled, solvable copy of the OBR’s published macroeconometric model, anchored to the latest EFO with held add-factors: shock a lever, re-solve, and read GDP, consumption and investment deviations against the official forecast path.

→Worked example: 1p on the basic rate — £6.46bn (2026) rising to £7.38bn (2030) with the macro feedback
→Emulator tracks the published forecast within 0.15% (GDP) / 0.25% (consumption)
→Impact multiplier ~1 by construction vs the OBR’s published 0.6 — read magnitudes, not point estimates

Open the OBR emulator →

Work, saving, and capital effects over decadesUK · psl-og

80 overlapping cohorts choose work and saving over their lifetimes; firms and the government close the economy. Solve the steady state or the full 60-period transition path and read where a reform takes GDP, investment and revenue in the long run.

→Worked example: +1pp basic rate → GDP −£5.0bn (−0.14%) and tax revenue +0.29pp of GDP by 2030
→Tax functions estimated from PolicyEngine microdata; ONS/OBR-calibrated
→Local-only and experimental: calibrated, with no published ground truth

Open OG-UK →

What drove UK GDP and inflation at the latest model-data vintageUK · boe-svar

An eight-variable Bayesian structural VAR identifies named shocks (demand, supply, policy…) with sign and zero restrictions, then decomposes recent GDP and inflation into their contributions — and projects forward with credible bands.

→Frozen-edge check: 14 of 14 outturns inside the 68% bands (bands run wide)
→Forecasts are archived before outturns exist and scored in public
→Hosted — same model behind the site’s UK forecast

How each model is validated.

The seven models support different kinds of checks; the gradient below groups them into four classes — open a model's validation page for the full evidence. In plain English: the OBR emulator tracks the published forecast within 0.15% on GDP, the SVAR's frozen-edge test put 14 of 14 outturns inside its 68% bands (single-origin, suggestive), and frb-us reproduces the Fed's database to machine precision.
