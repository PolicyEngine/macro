Taxes and benefits, for a household or a population

PolicyEngine microsimulation pe-microsim

UK + USexact for rules

Verdict: Exact for implemented rules (household maths); population estimates add survey error.

What it is, strengths and limits

PolicyEngine’s own engine, and the one behind policyengine.org: the tax-benefit rules as code over enhanced survey microdata, for one household or the whole population. The only member covering both countries.

→Household answers are exact rule arithmetic — the worked case checks with a pencil
→Population answers inherit survey-data error and carry no interval
→Static by design — no forecast, no GDP, inflation or behavioural response. The six below add those
→Hosted — MCP tools and a CLI, no account needed

A selected UK fiscal or macro scenario over 3–5 years

OBR emulator obr-macro

UKanchored

Verdict: Anchored to the EFO by construction, not validated as a forecaster; weak free-running.

What it is, strengths and limits

A transpiled, solvable copy of the OBR’s published macroeconometric model, anchored to the latest EFO: shock a lever, re-solve, and read GDP, consumption and investment against the official path.

→Tracks the published forecast by construction with the anchors held; weak free-running (validation)
→The household tax route transmits, but no quarter of the solve converges to tolerance; the spending lever’s multiplier is fixed by construction (limits)
→Joins the core: score_reform feeds the static costing in as an add-factor on household disposable income (HHDI_ADDFACTOR)

What drove UK GDP and inflation at the latest model-data vintage

BoE SVAR boe-svar

UKpartial replication

Verdict: Partial replication; GDP decomposition consistent with the paper, short on CPI; no forecast skill.

What it is, strengths and limits

An eight-variable Bayesian structural VAR: named shocks identified by sign and zero restrictions, a decomposition of recent GDP and inflation, and a forward projection with credible bands.

→Bands under-cover across 49 back-test origins; one frozen origin shows the opposite (coverage)
→Against a drifting random walk no skill survives once the tests are adjusted together (validation)
→Joins the core: the CPI path drives benefit-uprating incidence (svar_inflation_incidence, experimental)

A monetary or fiscal shock in the US over coming quarters

FRB/US frb-us

USreplicates

Verdict: Replicates the Fed’s pyfrbus on one 100bp funds-rate shock; a solver check, no predictive claim.

What it is, strengths and limits

The Federal Reserve’s FRB/US model — 284 equations parsed from the Board’s own model file — solved around the April 2026 LONGBASE baseline. Pick a lever and a policy rule, read the deviation path.

→Levers: federal funds rate, government spending, transfers, taxes
→Matches the Fed’s pyfrbus on a 100bp funds-rate shock, within the gap between the Fed’s own two releases (validation). Reproducing LONGBASE is an identity, not evidence
→Joins one way: wage and hours drive employment-income incidence (frbus_shock_incidence, experimental). score_reform refuses it

How US shock responses differ across the household wealth distribution

US HANK us-hank

USpartial replication

Verdict: Partial replication; six of seven solved parameters of the paper match; vphi is −17.3%, unresolved; not a forecast-accuracy claim.

What it is, strengths and limits

A two-asset heterogeneous-agent New Keynesian model (an ABRS 2021 replication): one monetary, fiscal or productivity shock, solved for the whole distribution of households.

→Consumption responses by liquid-wealth quartile and the hand-to-mouth share, warm in seconds from a cached sequence-space Jacobian
→Stylized replication — not a forecaster, no reform scoring, and responses are first-order
→Known model properties: MPCs well below US estimates, and a tax-financed spending rise that lowers output within a year (limits)
→Joins the core one way: wage and labour IRFs drive employment-income incidence (hank_shock_incidence, experimental)

Work, saving, and capital effects over decades

OG-UK psl-og

UKcalibrated

Verdict: Calibrated; no outcome benchmark.

What it is, strengths and limits

80 age cohorts choose work and saving over their lifetimes. Solve the steady state or the 60-period transition path and read where a reform takes GDP, investment and revenue in the long run.

→Worked example: +1pp on the basic rate, with the year-by-year path on the model page
→Local-only and experimental: calibrated, with no published ground truth
→Joins the core both ways: statute in through PolicyEngine-estimated tax functions, long-run changes back out into a second microsim run (og+microsim)

A UK climate-policy scenario, as deltas against a baseline

DEFINE-UK define-uk

UKpartial replication

Verdict: Partial replication; deltas only, never levels.

What it is, strengths and limits

The authors’ ecological stock-flow-consistent model of the UK economy and climate, run unmodified at a pinned commit: one published scenario, read as deltas over 2023–2037.

→Deltas only, never levels — the baseline sits far from outturns, so nothing here is a forecast
→Partial replication: the macro block replicates manual Table 4, and scenario deltas are checked only against the published scenario design (design-gated) because no numeric v1.1 scenario results are published
→Every published delta also carries a Baseline/policy parameter mismatch, so 2023–26 deltas are not policy effects (limits)
→Joins the core one way: scenario income deltas drive employment-income incidence (define_scenario_incidence, experimental)
