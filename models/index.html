Score a reform: microsimulation, then the OBR emulator

What would a reform cost, and what would it do to the economy?

Reform → Microsimulation → OBR emulator → Scored result 
pe-macro score --country uk --reform '{"gov.hmrc.income_tax.rates.uk[0].rate":0.21}' --model obr
About this join

Worked example: 1p on the basic rate, costed by the microsimulation and fed through the emulator; the figures live on the OBR Overview. The static costing runs below HMRC’s ready reckoner in the one matched year, 2026–27: a benchmark, not a validation. UK only (why a US reform gets no macro feedback).

Read the caveats before the headline: the costing inherits survey-microdata uncertainty, and the emulator’s spending multiplier is fixed by construction (limits), so treat macro feedback as sign and order of magnitude. Setup on the Use page.

About the engine and the macro members, and the same routes over MCP
  • The engine: PolicyEngine microsimulation, PolicyEngine's own microsimulation, the only member whose question types include household and the only one that covers both countries. It reports taxes, benefits, net income, revenue, distribution over a single policy year. The only two members that report a distribution are PolicyEngine microsimulation and psl-og+microsim, and the second is an overlay that runs the first.
  • The macro members supply what it cannot: GDP, inflation, interest rates and macro feedback. Four of the six routes put pe-microsim in the chain; two are a macro model standing alone.
  • Not a forecaster: pe-microsim’s question types are household, population, policy reform, without forecast; its predictive validation is recorded as not applicable. BoE SVAR reports posterior 68% and 90% intervals.
  • Over MCP the six routes are household_reform_impact, population_reform_impact, score_reform, dynamic_reform_impact, forecast_uk and svar_inflation_incidence; recommend_model routes a question to a member, or refuses when none supports it. Connect a client →
