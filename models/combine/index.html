How the models combine

Every join starts or ends at the microsimulation.

1

Score a reform: microsimulation, then the OBR emulator

What would a reform cost, and what would it do to the economy?

The microsimulation costs the reform from the tax-benefit rules; the OBR emulator returns the second-round effects.

Reform → Microsimulation → OBR emulator → Scored result 
pe-macro score --country uk --reform '{"gov.hmrc.income_tax.rates.uk[0].rate":0.21}' --model obr
About this join

Worked example: 1p on the basic rate, costed by the microsimulation and fed through the emulator; the figures live on the OBR Overview. The static costing runs below HMRC’s ready reckoner in the one matched year, 2026–27: a benchmark, not a validation. UK only (why a US reform gets no macro feedback).

Read the caveats before the headline: the costing inherits survey-microdata uncertainty, and the emulator’s spending multiplier is fixed by construction (limits), so treat macro feedback as sign and order of magnitude. Setup on the Use page.

About the engine and the macro members, and the same routes over MCP

The engine underneath this section is pe-microsim — PolicyEngine's own microsimulation, the only member whose question types include household and the only one that covers both countries. It applies a reform to household microdata and reports taxes, benefits, net_income, revenue, distribution over a single policy year. The only two members that report a distribution are pe-microsim and psl-og+microsim, and the second is an overlay that runs the first.

The macro members are what it cannot do on its own. pe-microsim cannot answer GDP, inflation, interest rates and macro feedback — so every macro number in this section comes from a member that produces one, and the table says which, in the order they run. Four of the six routes put pe-microsim in the chain; two are a macro model standing alone, which is also a fine thing to publish.

pe-microsim’s question types are household, population, policy_reform: forecast is not among them, and its predictive validation is recorded as not_applicable. The uncertainty of boe-svar is posterior 68% and 90% intervals.

Over MCP the same six routes are household_reform_impact, population_reform_impact, score_reform, dynamic_reform_impact, forecast_uk and svar_inflation_incidence; recommend_model routes a question to a member, or refuses when none supports it. Connect a client →
